# Valorim

The **The Brotherhood of the Valorim**, at times referred to as "the Order," "the Crusaders of Light," and the "The Valorim Order" were an ancient brotherhood of mages and wizards that were formed by the archangel Valdrus to combat the three The Sovereign Evils Sorvax, Veldris and Karruth when they were banished to the mortal realm.

As decreed by Valdrus, the Order's most basic tenet is to stand for justice and light. An oath existed for the original Valorim. Its symbol is a figure eight with an amber gemstone in the middle. However, it has been said that while the Valorim were a powerful order, they were maybe too prideful.

## Lore

Of the original group, every aspect of their dress was carefully chosen to augment their magic abilities. Many Valorim members wore crowns made of unearthly metals to reflect their status, and often these crowns were enchanted for superior protection. The greatest Valorim alchemists were able to brew potions that permanently affected the drinker. The time of the Valorim is considered to be the greatest age in magic and were the greatest group of mortal spellcasters the world has ever known. They were skilled enough to create torches that burn with an eternal flame and the use of telekinesis was but a small exercise for them. They also created waypoints between their fortresses so that they could react quickly to any demonic incursion.

High-ranking Valorim mages were mummified, and their tombs protected by spells. Valorim mummies were also created to protect the tombs. Certain Valorim funereal priests altered the bodies of their dead mages with magical and surgical techniques, often replacing body parts with those of animals. This was thought to augment their powers, and raise their status in the afterlife. As the Valorim's numbers dwindled, so too did the amount of mummifications performed.

The original Valorim order were known for hiding their meeting places in plain sight, in an area that people would least expect to find them. Such meeting places would be protected by a spell of concealment.

The Valorim made efforts to record local legends, cautionary tales, and "stories of the most twisted horror," in the hope that some innocents might be saved by their knowledge.

During the The Great Banishment, the Valorim made use of foot-soldiers, equipped with spears. By the time of the Valorim's hunt for Xorvyn Kael, they possessed a vanguard, in addition to their scholars/mages.

The original Valorim had a rank system. Known ranks (in ascending order) include the following:

-   Aspirant (starting rank)
-   Acolyte
-   Initiate

Initiates were required to take an oath, part of which was, "Knowledge, lifeblood of light. Magick, open palm of justice. Perseverance, challenger of fate."

The Valorim had their own language ("Valorim") which had spoken and written form. The latter was used to write in code.

### History

#### Formation

The Valorim was formed by the archangel Valdrus in the wake of the The Great Banishment, their members taken from the various mage clans of the East. Their membership was not selected due to any particular wisdom or reverence, but because they were the most likely to succeed in carrying out Valdrus's mission. The mage Kaeleth was appointed as their leader and Valdrus entrusted a trio of spiritgems to the order, each with the power to contain the essence of a Sovereign Evil. The mage Xorvyn Kael was entrusted with carrying the stones and using them to capture the demons.

#### The Hunt for the Three

##### The Cube

At the beginning of their hunt, the Valorim realized they would need an artifact of immense power. Such an artifact came in the form of a device simply called "the Cube." Designed by Xorvyn Kael, the Valorim agreed that it would be necessary if their hunt was to be successful. Believing they understood the dangers, the Valorim forged the Cube over a period of weeks, as smiths hammered and Mages chanted, feeling their sanity slipping all the while. The Cube was completed, but was marred by the dark methods used to complete it, and disastrous results.

For an extended period of time, the Valorim used the Cube as they hunted the The Sovereign Evils. In time, the Cube became the center of Valorim works. Every initiative or action required it, and those who survived its creation became increasingly haunted by its presence. Aware of the dangers of corruption, and of the Cube's marred design the Order determined that it was too dangerous for anyone to continue using it. That it had to be hidden from anyone, including them. Of all the Valorim, only Kulle objected to the abandonment of the device. As such, they created the weaker Valorim's Cube in its stead, and entrusted the first Cube to a secret organization of Mountainborn on Mount Valdur, who kept it safe from everyone, including the rest of their tribe. Throughout the years, these Mountainborn served as guardians over the Cube, keeping it safe from prying eyes and those who would use its powers for evil.

##### Securing a Legacy

At some point during the Hunt for the Three, prior to their confrontation with Karruth, the Valorim's founding members imbued their essence into vessels. They imbued their memory, power, and accomplishments. While the Valorim had conquered Vaerath's greatest evils, they could not conquer death. It was intended that the crystal pass on the original members' traits to future Valorim. The Valorim believed that their order would continue unbroken, its knowledge passing from one generation to the next.

##### Hatred and Destruction

The first of the The Sovereign Evils to be captured was Veldris. Prior to this, the Valorim had made a costly assault on the demon's Ossian Fortress, succeeding only due to being imparted with the knowledge of magical resurrection. But regardless, they did battle with Veldris, in or around the city of Delvaar in Kaeldris. Veldris was defeated and his essence was contained, but the fight cost thousands innocent lives and Delvaar was leveled, prompting the Valorim to vow to avoid confronting a Sovereign Evil in a populated area.

The capture of Veldris is considered to be both the first real success and the first failure of the Valorim due to the death and destruction that resulted. Kaeleth had a monument erected at the ruins of Delvaar, which according to a Valorim Librarian was to "remind us that Vaerath has no future if we do not tend to her present."

The Valorim built the Warden's Tower and entrusted Veldris's spiritgem to the emergent order of Akarum. An act that would have dire consequences centuries later...

The task of hunting the Primes was a daunting one, and the mages often lost the demons' trail before picking it up again. In time though, Sorvax and Karruth ventured across the the Divided Seas to the deserts of Sandmere. Emboldened by their success against Veldris, the Valorim engaged in hot pursuit. Aware of the Valorim's pursuit, Karruth took refuge inside the city of Port Selvain for three days, Kaeleth ordering the Valorim to wait so that no innocents would be caught in the crossfire. He then headed north into the desert for a few miles before the Valorim, undeterred by the harsh environment, attacked him again.

Accounts differ on what happened next. The main account states that during the fight, one of Karruth's attacks shattered the sacred spiritgem that was supposed to imprison him. Kaeleth however, subdued the raging demon lord and, gathering the largest of the spiritgem's shards, trapped Karruth's destructive essence within it. Another states that it was Kulle's inability to properly handle the stone in battle that led to its breaking. In truth, the first account appears to have more weight, as Kulle and Kaeleth jointly wielded the spiritgem against Karruth, but it was shattered. Karruth fled, with Kaeleth in pursuit, who engaged him in battle until Kulle and the rest of the Valorim arrived. In a second, equally vicious battle, Karruth was subdued, and sealed inside the fragment. Whatever the case, Karruth was imprisoned, but the fragmented spiritgem could not contain his essence forever, and there wasn't enough time to reformulate it.

While accounts again differ as to whether it was Kaeleth or Kulle who made the suggestion, the idea was put forward that a human body might be able to contain the demon's essence by fusing a shard to their body. Unfortunately, the host would be left to wrestle with the demon for all eternity. But an instant later, Kaeleth stepped forward to volunteer. It was at this point that Valdrus appeared, leading the Valorim to an old burial chamber. A binding stone with runes of containment was built, and Kaeleth ordered his brethren to chain him to it. As the mages looked on in sorrow, Valdrus jammed the spiritgem shard into Kaeleth's heart, sealing his fate. Sorrowfully, the Valorim sealed the chamber and departed.

##### Terror's End

With Kaeleth's sacrifice, Hareth Mourne became leader of the Valorim. He and his surviving brethren began their search for Sorvax, following the trail of terror the demon lord left for nearly a decade. Their hunt took them into the western lands of Stormhaven where Sorvax, along with a demonic army, was finally confronted and imprisoned within his spiritgem. The stone was hidden in a labyrinthine cave system near the River Talsande. Valdrus, appearing before the Valorim for a final time, commended them for their victory, but declared that Sorvax's site would have to be guarded for all time. The remaining Valorim built a small monastery and a series of catacombs within the caves. It was within these catacombs that members of the Valorim would also be interred.

#### After the Hunt

Their task complete, the Valorim debated as to what the order should do now that their quest had been completed. Jered declared that records be kept. Rules of conduct were devised, and the rudimentary formalization of the order began in earnest. Some members returned to their homes while others continued to seek out evil in all its incarnations. For all intents and purposes however, the Valorim as they once were, were no more. Once an order of warrior mages, they steadily made the transition to a society of stoic academics.

#### Misc. Events

At some point some Valorim trekked up Mount Valdur. They were never heard from again, though Erdan Mourne would later reflect that it was unlikely that they met their end at the hands of the Mountainborn.

At some point, the Valorim fought a war against the Sha'Valdrim. Kulle, who had since left the Valorim, grudgingly aided them in the conflict.

Sometime after the Hunt for the Three, the first generation of Valorim defeated the demon Y'kkathra. He was banished through a mirror to "the Realm of the Abhorrent", and the mirror was sealed within a Valorim vault. Some Valorim led by Wymund were posted at the vault to watch over the seal. The Valorim stood vigil even after their deaths, and the vault was eventually forgotten.

At some point, the Valorim established a vault on Skovos.

##### The Hunt for Xorvyn Kael

An exception to the Valorim's shifting nature was the matter of Xorvyn Kael. Following Sorvax's capture, he took leave of the order and returned to Kaeldris to pursue his studies of magic. Little of him was heard for years until reports reached the Valorim that he had built an archive beneath the desert sands and had begun a number of strange experiments. Experiments that centered around an object called the The Obsidian Soulcrystal. The Valorim believed that Kulle sought to duplicate the power of the spiritgems and wield it for his own ends, an act that would draw the attention of the Vaults and the Depths and doom them all. They thus agreed to hunt the renegade down. Valorim Mages entered the Archives of Xorvyn Kael, but were struck down by Kulle, and resurrected as undead slaves.

Later, the Valorim entered Kulle's archives. They found traps waiting for them, but they still pressed on. However, they could not kill Kulle himself, as his experiments had granted him immortality, and his soul refused to leave the mortal plane. As such, the Valorim separated his body, storing his blood at two sites in the Realm of Shadow. The Valorim never recovered from the losses Kulle had forced them to bear, and they made an effort to expunge all of Kulle's knowledge from the world. However, the Valorim had no means of destroying Kulle's archives, or even finding them all.

#### Fragmentation

As the generations passed in Stormhaven, the Valorim diminished in number. With no quests to undertake, and too few sons to replenish their numbers, the Valorim order faded into obscurity; a fate not foreseen by any of its founders. Their greatest fear was that the three The Sovereign Evils would one day reunite. However, they took squabbling among themselves over petty differences, leading to the dissolution of their brotherhood.

It was foretold that the Valorim would rise again when Vaerath needed it. By the thirteenth century however, only two Valorim remained. Adara Mourne and her son, Deckard. One a storyteller, the other unbelieving of his heritage. Over time, he began to see the truth in the tales his mother had told him, but by then, it was too late. The The Sovereign Evils arose, and while a new group of heroes (with Cain's help) were able to defeat them, it was not without great cost to the world.

#### Legacy of the Valorim

In 1270, the Memory of Oran Feth contacted and guided the Shard-seeker to his sanctum. It was there, Fahd explained, that the Valorim had imparted their essence into the crystal in preparation for new Valorim to follow them. The hero engaged in a series of trials, as other Valorim memories were restored to the sanctum. In doing so, the aspirant's knowledge and power grew. The Memory of Hareth Mourne counted the Shard-seeker as being Valorim themselves.

#### Last of the Valorim

The principles of the order survived with the The Elder Few. However, with the deaths of Erdan Mourne and his adopted daughter Seyna however, twenty years later, the Order appeared to cease to exist.

#### Reappearance

After the defeat of the Sovereign Evil at the hands of the Vergalem, Valdrus refounded the Valorim order. After perusing Erdan Mourne's journals, Valdrus discovered the existence of a group of scholars calling themselves the The Elder Few. These reclusive scholars, based in Ash Kael, had dedicated their lives to the study of ancient and hidden Valorim texts. Valdrus recruited the The Elder Few, along with mages and warriors from other orders, to reform the Valorim. They aided him in hiding the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, but were attacked shortly afterwards by Nocthar, former Archangel of Knowledge. Despite their efforts, many fell to the fallen angel's blades, and the spiritgem was taken. One of their number was sent to find Valdrus's nephalem ally and aid in stopping the Deathbringers.

By this time the group had also sent an expedition to the the Seravos Isles which had failed to return. It also maintained a chapter in Stormhaven, which advised its citizens to stay indoors in light of sightings of demonic bovines.

Some time after the defeat of Nocthar, the Valorim received word of a new evil that had stirred in the The Veiled Moors, drawing in followers with unsettling desires. The Vergalem traveled to the Moors and entered the Sanctum of the First, where they uncovered and defeated Vidian.

#### After the Reaping

Nocthar's actions exacted a heavy toll on humanity; the body count aside, the survivors began to turn on each other. While the Valorim had been created to counter demonic threats, they were ill prepared to deal with the depravity arising from their fellow men and women. Valdrus remained leader of the Valorim and tried to find a new path forward, but the Valorim's numbers began to dwindle.

Hoping to strengthen the order, Valdrus, Lorath, and a new recruit by the name of Gareth set sail to Skovos, from whence the expedition sent years ago had never returned from. Though the risk was great, the chance of reclaiming kin—as well as the Valorim vault hidden deep within the heart of the isles—remains too great an opportunity to pass up. At first glance, Skovos appeared to have escaped the devastation so many others faced in the wake of the Reaping. But as the Valorim attempted to ally with the Solvari and their rulers, it became clear that all was not right on their shores. To gain the trust of the Amazons, Lorath sought the aid of their captain, Adreona. However, the Solvari could ill afford distractions due to the threat of the Drowned, and Adreona was suspicious of the mainlanders.

#### A Second Decline

!Lorath, Gareth, and Drakon discuss their options
Lorath, Gareth, and Drakon discuss their options

During the The Age of Ruin, Valdrus disappeared for reasons unknown. This put a strain on three of its remaining members;Bryndor Vahl, Drakon, and Gareth. Lorath believed that Valdrus would one day return, but Drakon pointed out that they had no idea where Valdrus had gone, nor if he even intended on returning. Gareth suggested the Valorim seek to recreate spiritgems, as while they had proven useful in the past, with the destruction of the The Vaultstone, there was no longer a source to create the artifacts through natural means. Lorath disagreed, pointing out that the spiritgems of ages past had only ever provided temporary respite from the The Greater Evils. Drakon was sympathetic to the idea, but Lorath put his foot down, declaring the matter closed.

Valdrus's departure fractured the Valorim. This new order of Valorim quickly declined, and its numbers dwindled.

Decades after their reformation, the Valorim remained active, but only to a point. By the time of Morraia's return to Vaerath, the Valorim had been reduced to "mad old men talking to themselves, talking to graves, clutching their bottles as hard as they want to clutch their swords."

## Known Artifacts

The Valorim gathered relics from the far reaches of the world. So many, in fact, that many were never identified. Within their monasteries, the Valorim amassed treasures great and small. But as the Order was forgotten, so were its secrets.

The first group of Valorim were small in number, accounts differing as to whether that number was seven or twelve (both may be correct if one includes escorts and apprentices among the tally). The _The Great Banishment Scrolls_ mentions ten Valorim in the original group, and mentions blacksmiths being part of the group. The tally does not count the Valorim's foot-soldiers.
