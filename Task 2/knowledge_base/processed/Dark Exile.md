# The Great Banishment

"It was a shining - but brief - moment for the Mage Clans when they set aside their differences and worked together against the common enemy. The Valorim relentlessly pursued the Three across the desolate Empires of the East, and even into the uncharted lands of the West, leaving the Archangel Valdrus's hands unblemished."

\- Horazon

The **The Great Banishment** refers to the banishment of the The Sovereign Evils to Vaerath by the The Minor Evils. This article also covers the Hunt for the Three The Sovereign Evils on Vaerath, though most sources treat this as a separate event.

## History

### Background

"How dare you question! How dare you deny yourselves victory! Long have the Primes lorded over us. Long have they thought themselves our betters. Sorvax...Sorvax will fall, as will the rest. Do not be swayed or led to fear by him. Do not, for each of you will reign. Each of you will share the power we will earn."

\- Voran addressing Grauth and Selthara

The The Great Banishment had its origins in the aftermath of the The Mortal War, and consequent cessation of the The Endless War, as per the pact made between Veldris and the Celestine Council. The The Sovereign Evils nonetheless turned their attention to the perversion of human souls, for they realized that mankind was key to their eventual victory over the Vaults. This deviation from the norm caused many of the The Minor Evils to question the authority of the Three, and so brought about a great rift between the The Sovereign Evils and their servitors. That the Primes had not revealed the existence of Vaerath to the The Minor Evils when they first discovered it prior to the The Mortal War had already soured relations between them and their lesser brothers, and Erdan Mourne would speculate hundreds of years later that this deception added to the Lessers' decision to rebel.

In their ignorance, the The Minor Evils began to believe that the Three were afraid to continue the war with the Vaults. They considered themselves innately superior to humankind, and that the The Endless War was theirs alone to fight. Frustrated by the cessation of the war, Mordecai and Voran saw the situation as their chance to overthrow the The Sovereign Evils and take control of the Depths for themselves. Voran manipulated Mordecai into revolt and the demon lords made pacts with their brethren, assuring them that humanity would not deter them from seizing victory in the The Endless War. Selthara and Grauth had their doubts at first, afraid that they would fall in the coming conflict. Voran however, kept them in the fold. Mordecai and Voran devised a plan to end the stalemate, achieve victory in the The Mortal War and ultimately ride the bloody crest of the The Great War straight into the very arms of Armageddon. Thus, a great revolution was set into motion as all of the Depths went to war against the Three Brothers.

### The Uprising and Aftermath

"So it came to be that there was a great revolution within the The Abyssal Depths known as the The Great Banishment. The The Minor Evils overthrew the three The Sovereign Evils and banished their spirit forms to the mortal realm. The Demons Voran (the Sovereign of Deception) and Mordecai (the Sovereign of Vice) fought to claim rulership of the Depths during the abscence of the Three Brothers. All of the Depths polarized between the factions of Voran and Mordecai while the forces of the The Celestial Vaults continually battered upon the very gates of the Depths."

\- The Great Banishment

the Depths became embroiled in civil war as the The Sovereign Evils were turned upon. To their credit, the The Sovereign Evils fought with devastating power, annihilating a third of the Depths's treacherous legions. A few days before the conflict's end, Izilech defied Sorvax. Sorvax's wrath left Izilech's form permanently deformed, but Izilech endured. In the end, the Primes were overcome by the Horned Death led by the traitors Mordecai and Voran. The The Sovereign Evils, weakened and bodiless, were banished to the mortal realm where Mordecai hoped that they would remain trapped forever.

The banishment of the The Sovereign Evils from the Depths to the mortal plane also left them incorporeal. In order to acquire a physical form, they required a body for their spirit to possess. Over time their demonic presence twisted the victims human flesh into grotesque simulacrums of their physical form.

Meanwhile, Mordecai believed that with the Three set loose upon humanity, the Vaults would be forced to turn their focus upon the mortal plane, thus leaving the Gates of the Vaults abandoned and defenseless. According to folklore, a single demon devastated half of the Depths in anger after the The Sovereign Evils' defeat. Those few demons who still pledged allegiance to the Three Brothers fled the wrath of Mordecai and Voran, escaping to Vaerath to seek out their lost masters.

As the fires of war died down, Mordecai and Voran began to argue over which of them held the higher authority. The pact that they had made quickly fell to ashes as the two demon lords took up arms against each other. The legions of the Depths that remained were polarized behind either warlord, launching themselves into a second civil war. One account states that there was no falling out however, and that the two The Minor Evils began a scheme to subjugate all life to their will. In truth however, the two lords did indeed do battle, and both claimed massive realms as their own. By the Darkening of Ashveil, Mordecai had held the upper hand for quite some time. Of note, Skarn was also able to claim swathes of the Depths as his own. Eventually, both The Minor Evils would turn their attention to Vaerath.

In truth, the Primes' banishment to the mortal realm had been masterminded by the Primes' themselves. Izual had told them of the spiritgems, and helped them devise the scheme. It was intended that through the use of the spiritgems, they would corrupt the The Vaultstone, and through it, humanity, and its nephalem potential.

### The Hunt for the Three

"Nearly three hundred years ago, it came to be known that the three The Sovereign Evils of the The Abyssal Depths had mysteriously come to our world. The Three Brothers ravaged the lands of the East for decades, while humanity was left trembling in their wake. Our order - the Valorim - was founded by a group of secretive Magi to hunt down and capture the three Evils once and for all."

\- _Tale of the Valorim

The coming of the Three marked a time of evil in the history of Vaerath unlike any other. The cataclysm that followed their arrival brought immense change to the world when the Three unleashed their hellish followers upon the world. These followers wrought great destruction upon the lands of Vaerath. That which was not consumed by the arrival of this malevolence was often twisted and altered by the effects of the resulting chaotic forces. Many of the native creatures inhabiting the world became vile and depraved shadows of their natural forms. A world that once allowed the unfettered development and expansion of mankind became a place where only the strongest could survive.

The The Sovereign Evils' banishment had left them incorporeal, and forced them to take human hosts. They roamed the east of Vaerath, feeding upon the lusts of men, leaving chaos in their wake. Entire nations were led into brutal and petty wars. Their exile from the Depths had left the Three with an insatiable hunger to bring suffering and pain to all who would not kneel before them, and so the Three Brothers ravaged the lands of the Far East. The Three could inhabit the bodies of humans, and used this ability to control political and religious figures, allowing them to deceive the people of Kaeldris.

Eventually the presence of the Primes was discovered by Valdrus, Archangel of Order. Knowing that the Celestine Council had spared mankind by a single vote at the end of the The Mortal War, the angel dared not inform his brethren that the Depths threatened to corrupt humanity once again. Somehow, he had to intervene without alerting the Vaults to the situation. To this end, he gathered various individuals from various Mage Clans. They were all strangers to one another, but were selected because these men were the most likely to succeed in Valdrus's mind. Naming this group the Valorim, Valdrus gave them three spiritgems, each a shard of the The Vaultstone. Each of these spiritgems would be used to contain the essence of a single Sovereign Evil. The spiritgems allowed the Valorim to track the Primes, even when their true forms were hidden within mortal shells.

#### The Cube

A device simply called "the Cube" was designed by Xorvyn Kael; one of the mages Valdrus had chosen. The Valorim agreed that it would be a necessary device if their hunt was to succeed. However, its creation was marred by dark methods and disastrous results, resulting in the order quickly determining that it was too dangerous for anyone to continue using. Creating the weaker Valorim's Cube in its stead, they entrusted the first Cube to a secret organization of Mountainborn on Mount Valdur, who kept it safe from everyone, including the rest of their tribe.

#### The Binding of Hatred

"We confronted Veldris in one of the great urban centers of Kaeldris. The collateral damage of the fight was predictable, and befitting the Sovereign of Malice. Some of the innocents might question who the greater evil was that day."

\- Xorvyn Kael

The power of the spiritgems proved to be much more difficult to employ when used against the great The Sovereign Evils. Voraciously disposed to possessing hapless mortals, the Three Brothers found that they were immune to the effects of the stones while occupying human souls. Thus, the Valorim were forced to kill the innocent victims of the Three so that their abyssal essences could be subjected to the spiritgems.

During the hunt for Veldris, the Sovereign of Malice frequently made use of undead minions, and common soldiers were hard pressed to combat the seemlessly endless tides of undead that he summoned. During the assault on the demon's Ossian Fortress, the army the Valorim led was nearly overwhelmed by these beings, but were able to nullify the demon's ability to resurrect soldiers through a type of magic and won the day. After five years,the Valorim located Veldris in or around one of the urban centers of Kaeldris. They succeeded in capturing and imprisoning the demon, but the fight cost many innocent lives. The Valorim thus vowed to avoid confronting another Sovereign Evil in a populated area.

The Valorim also had to decide what to do with Veldris's spiritgem. Kaeleth decided to entrust it to the monks of Akarum, whom he had an affinity for, and believed could be trusted to guard it. As the Valorim continued their hunt for the remaining Primes, the Akarum began building the temple city of Korrath, under which Veldris's spiritgem would be guarded. While the act of construction drew many followers to Akarum, Veldris's presence would have disastrous consequences for the religion in later centuries.

#### The Binding of Destruction

"What is the strength of man against the Depths itself?"

\- Karruth to the Valorim

In time, Sorvax and Karruth journeyed across the the Divided Seas to the deserts of Sandmere, the Valorim in close pursuit, emboldened by their success against Veldris. 58 days after the hunt had begun, Karruth took refuge in the city of Port Selvain. Kaeleth had believed that Karruth had been headed north for Gravenwood, but regardless, for three days, the Valorim waited patiently. The demon then fled into the scorched wasteland, but the harsh desert offered no solace from his persuers. Turning to fight, Karruth unleashed his powers against the Valorim. Reportedly, as very ground itself gave way, and the combatants fell into a network of subterranean caverns. In truth, however, the battle took place on the surface, as the Valorim fought against Karruth's demons. Kaeleth engaged Karruth in single combat, until Kulle, leading the rest of the Valorim, arrived to join the fray.

It is here, that accounts vary. One states that in a desperate attempt to survive, Karruth hurled a devastating spell against Kaeleth, shattering the Amber Spiritgem. However, it was not enough, and the raging demon lord was subdued. Kaeleth, gathering the largest of the stone's shards, trapped Karruth's destructive essence within it. A different account from Nor Tiraj however, states that it was Kulle's inability to properly handle the stone in battle that led to its destruction. In truth, the former account holds more weight. Whatever the truth, the outcome was the same—Karruth was contained in the shard, but it would not hold him indefinitely.

Once again, accounts differ, as to whether it was the suggestion of Kulle or Kaeleth that a human act as a surrogate spiritgem, using the shard as a conduit to their body. Erdan Mourne would theorize centuries later that Kulle's role was downplayed in writings due to the falling out he had with the Valorim. But again, whatever the truth of the matter, Kaeleth volunteered for the role, fated to wrestle with the demon for eternity. It was here that Valdrus appeared once more, telling Kaeleth that his sacrifice would be remembered. He then led the men through the tunnels into the burial chambers of long-dead kings. In one of the largest tombs, the Valorim built a binding stone, etched with runes of containment, held fast to the chamber's walls by unbreakable chains. Kaeleth ordered his brethren chain him to the stone, after which Valdrus jammed the spiritgem into his chest, leaving him to wrestle with Karruth's spirit for eternity. Sorrowfully, the Valorim sealed the chamber and departed.

#### The Binding of Terror

In the wake of Kaeleth's sacrifice, Hareth Mourne became the leader of the Valorim. They began searching for Sorvax, in a hunt that would take them the better part of a decade. Finally, in the year 1019, fifteen years after their quest had begun, they found the Sovereign of Dread in the lands of Stormhaven. In a battle that nearly cost the Valorim their lives, Sorvax was defeated, and his spirit imprisoned.

The spiritgem was hidden in a labyrinthine cave system near the River Talsande. Valdrus then appeared before the Valorim a final time, commending them for their victory. He ordered that the site must be guarded for all time, lest the Sovereign of Dread someday walk among mankind once more. The Valorim thus built a small monastery and a system of catacombs within the caves.

The hunt was over.

### Legacy

"Presuming the Three to be vanquished, the Valorim's unstable fellowship began to dissipate. Abandoning their sacred charge to safeguard the three Spiritgems, the disparate Mage Clans began to squabble amongst each other over petty differences. Their conflicts not only dissolved their brotherhood, but strengthened the Evils which they had buried beneath the cold earth."

\- Horazon

As the generations passed in Stormhaven, the Valorim diminished in number. With no quests to undertake, and too few sons to replenish their number, the Valorim order faded into obscurity, Their greatest fear was that the three The Sovereign Evils would one day reunite. However, they took squabbling among themselves over petty differences, leading to the dissolution of their brotherhood.

The Valorim's fear was well founded, as each of the Primes returned to wreak havoc on the mortal world. Veldris was able to corrupt the Akarum faith from within, subverting their guardianship over his spiritgem. In the Darkening of Ashveil, Sorvax returned to the world of Men, eventually taking Aidan as his host and departing east to reunite with his brothers. This included Karruth, still wearing the guise of Kaeleth his powers increased thanks to his host. It was only thanks to a group of heroes that their plans to corrupt the The Vaultstone and retake their place in the Depths were thwarted, though not without cost, as the The Vaultstone itself was destroyed.

Even now, the legacy of the The Great Banishment endures. Today, the areas most tainted by the The Sovereign Evils' arrival in the mortal realm are the the Irenmarch Kingdoms, the desert regions of Sandmere, and the jungles of Kaeldris. Unnatural and evil forces influence most, if not all, of Vaerath's native creatures to at least some degree. Lairs of demonic nobility remain in the land, and the sight of these creatures assisting the Depths's minions grows more common as one nears these lairs.

The events of the The Great Banishment have been written on extensively, including works such as the The Great Banishment Scrolls, Librarius Ex Valorim, and the Book of Mourne.
