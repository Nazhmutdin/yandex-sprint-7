# Vaerath

"This is the place we know as the mortal realm. This is our world. We must pause a moment to think upon this. Our world, unlike all the other worlds, was created by both angels and demons."

**Vaerath**, otherwise known as the **The Mortal Veil**, or the **The Mortal Veil** is the primary setting for the _Sorvax_ series.

## Lore

"The world around us is a vault of riches waiting to be plundered. Every creature, every plant, and every culture can serve us, willingly or not, in our goal to aid the Lords of the The Abyssal Depths."

### Geography

"This is Vaerath. To protect it, you must cross many lands. Where forgotten evils linger. Yet you must go without fear. Into the heart of the unknown. This burden falls to us. A chance to fight the darkness. To ensure our home's survival."

Vaerath is a large world, and a harsh, dark, and unforgiving one. Filled with horrific monsters, one must be merciless toward their enemies if they are to survive in this world. The many bloodlusted creatures that burrow beneath the many hovels and bogs of the world may seem mindless, but they have not been eradicated, as they have mutated and adapted over time, becoming more formidable. Some of the Depths's most dangerous creatures have made their way into this world.

Even the wealthiest areas in Vaerath are challenging to exist in. As a land plagued by darkness, upholding a semblance of justice in Vaerath is seemingly an impossible task.

The forces that shape Vaerath are unknown to most of its denizens. Its biomes range from frigid mountains to sand-swept canyons.

A number of focal points exist within Vaerath; places that have been "hammered down," that hold Vaerath's reality together. This is due to the nature of its creation by both angels and demons, who combined their powers into these focal points. Similarly, sites of power exist across Vaerath, and leyveins transmit energy between these sites. The leyveins drift, and are difficult to follow. It is common for humans to erect shrines or altars on these sites, and while these structures come and go, the sites themselves remain.

Vaerath has seasons such as spring, autumn and winter; the latter two occuring late in Vaerath's calendar year. Harvest is carried out in the former, in order to survive the rigors of the latter. On the last day of harvest season, the wisest folk lock their doors, hang their wards, hide under their beds, and wait for dawn. Winter can be harsh, but those who survive the winter fever retain the strength of the madness it brings. The closer the year moves to winter, the longer the nights become.

More of Vaerath is covered by ocean, whose volatile nature have claimed the lives and souls of many foolhardy adventurers. Maps of Vaerath depict two major landmasses, separated by the the Divided Seas. The eastern landmass is called Valdoran, otherwise known as the the Valdoran Continent. This is boarded by the the Amber Sea, while the western landmass is bordered by the the Vastian Sea. To the north of both continents lies the the Glacial Sea. The seas of Vaerath are vast and volatile, and have claimed the lives of countless foolhardy adventurers.

Vaerath's landscape has been altered a lot over time, especially due to the The Endless War, the destruction of Mount Valdur being one of the more famous examples. During the The Reign of Ruin, Abyss Tides repeatedly morphed Vaerath's landscape.

#### Astro-geography

"The Taan magi have long believed that, through the stars above, we see the realms beyond. The movements of the astral leave invisible patterns across the land—traces of the power that moves through our world. There's no disputing that reality... but isn't it curious that their "ley lines" are so difficult to map it in, as though the ashes of Arreat have dirtied the sky, and the light of the stars can barely reach us, let alone be interpreted. Or perhaps there's a simpler answer: the Taan are delusional, and they've put their faith in a tradition they barely understand. That seems more likely."

Vaerath resides in a pocket dimension, separate from both the Vaults and the Depths,and surrounded by the The Veil Realm akin to a veil. The Priests of Aethon believe that Vaerath lies upon the back of Trag'Oul, and Lintel XII maintained the existence of a "world disc," along the circumference of which is the serpent Kepeleke. In contrast, the Celestial Court holds that Vaerath is a spherical world. The world possesses a single sun, a single moon and beyond Vaerath lie stars.

Both the moon and stars can affect the practice of magic within Vaerath. The stars themselves are grouped into constellations such as the phoenix. Stars may fade and plummet to Vaerath akin to bolides. The Keth Clan believe that through the stars, realms beyond Vaerath can be observed.

Vaerath has solstices and equinoxes, and the alignment of heavenly bodies beyond Vaerath generate power due to the harmonic nature of Creation. The moon sometimes moves in front of the sun, causing an eclipse.

Vaerath experiences an atmospheric phenomenon known as aurora borealis. Its endless shimmer, an untamable dance, is best admired from afar. For those who cross this unspoken boundary are upended by a most heinous curse.

At times, over the centuries, Vaerath and the Depths touch in a manner that allows the summoning of demons into the mortal realm to be easier than it might otherwise be. Similarly, the glory of the the Vaultss sometimes verges onto Vaerath, turning hidden places into hallowed ground.

#### Lands of the West

Sandmere lies on western shore of the the Divided Seas, with the Tamoe Mountain Range separating it from the lands of the West. These lands are collectively known as the the Irenmarch Kingdoms, and consist of Stormhaven, Varenteg, and Irenmarch. Off the coast of Irenmarch lies a series of islands, one of which is Ashhollow Island. The north most island is Tempestfall.

In the north of the western continent lies the The Blightlands, formerly known as the The Northern Marches. To the east of this realm lies Storveld, though as a country, these realm no longer exists bar a single city-state.

South of the western continent lies the the Seravos Isles, consisting of four main islands with the names of Philios, Skovos, Lycander, and Skartara.

The northern Part of the ocean to the west of the western continent is the the Lost Sea; and it is filled with surging tides and fog.

#### Lands of the East

The eastern continent is biodiverse, and prior to its collapse, was dominated by the empire of Kaeldris, which encompassed a full third of Vaerath's eastern hemisphere. The area covered by Kaeldris can broadly be defined by its northern desert and southern rainforests. To its north lies the The Ashen Steppes, and further still, Gravenwood. Off Gravenwood's western coast lies the island of Xiansai.

To Kaeldris's southwest lies the Draveth Jungles, and bordering the the Divided Seas, the Marshland. To the far east lies the Swampland.

#### The the World's Crown

Beyond Vaerath's main continents—at its north pole, lies the the World's Crown, commonly called the The Crown of All. It is a barren land devoid of life, and has been described as a blank spot in the canvas of creation.

#### Lands Unknown

Lands and countries are known to exist/have existed whose locations are unclear. These included the now-defunct Valdrath Empire, the nation of Korenth, the island nation of Uie, and the Solveth Jungle.

### History

Vaerath has always been a deadly world—even before the coming of demons. It possesses a bloody history, filled with struggle and conflict. A number of its casualties have been lost to history.

#### Origins

"Aelorin and his new companions altered the frequency or dimensional alignment of the The Vaultstone, using its power to conceal it from the angels and demons still fighting the The Endless War. They shifted the massive crystal into a pocket dimension, and there they shaped a garden paradise around it: a world of refuge they would call Vaerath."

Vaerath has its origins with Aelorin, Morraia, and the renegade angels and demons who followed them, seeking an escape from the The Endless War. They absconded with the The Vaultstone from the The Chaos Citadel and shifted it into a pocket dimension. There, they used its extraordinary power to shape a new world. A refuge free from war, free from unending strife. A Vaerath.

The coupling of angels and demons resulted in the creation of the nephalem. The nephalem spread across Vaerath, and their parents realized that their children were more powerful than they. This raised concerns among the renegades, who not only feared the nephalem's power in of itself, but the risk that their offspring might draw the attention of the Vaults and the Depths. Many of the renegades held the opinion that the nephalem be destroyed, while others believed their children should be spared. Aelorin, troubled by the dissension, called for a period of reflection. A period that was broken by Morraia as, maddened by the threat of her children's extinction, turned on the renegade angels and demons.

Morraia was defeated by Aelorin, but not before she'd killed almost every angel and demon that had followed them. Enraged, Aelorin still couldn't bring himself to slay Morraia, and thus banished her to the The Hollow Between. Aelorin then attuned the The Vaultstone to cause the powers of the nephalem to diminish over time. Thus led to the subsequent weakening of the nephalem and emergence of their descendants—humanity.

#### The The Mortal War

"You are all deceived, my brothers. A new age has already begun. Can you not sense them? Ugly creatures, born in shadow. They feel terror, hatred, and the desire to destroy. Yes, but they are not ours yet. They will open their world to us very soon. An invitation we cannot refuse."

Over the subsequent millennia, the nephalem faded from Vaerath, becoming the origin of various myths. Humanity, however, spread across Vaerath, ignorant of its origins. However, because of the actions of Duren Karash, the Depths was made aware of the existence of the mortal realm. The The Sovereign Evils, sensing the dormant power of the nephalem within these mortal beings, resolved to corrupt humanity to turn into a weapon against the Vaults. To gain followers, the The Three-Faced was founded—a seemingly benevolent religion that was in reality a front for this darker purpose. The The Three-Faced spread in number and influence across Kehjan, with only its highest echelons knowing the truth behind the faith.

Aelorin, aware of what the The Three-Faced really was, and afraid of what would happen if the Vaults discovered humanity's existence in the same way the Depths had, formed the Spire of Dawn to counter the The Three-Faced's influence. Thus began the The Mortal War, as the two faiths warred to claim the hearts of humankind. This spiraled into open conflict as Morraia returned to Vaerath, awakening the powers of the nephalem in a mortal named Aldric val-Oreth. Morraia sought to rid Vaerath of the influence of both Aelorin and the Depths, but she was willing to sacrifice as many lives as necessary to make that happen. After discovering Morraia's true nature, Aldric refused to be her pawn, and turned his wrath against both the The Three-Faced and Cathedral. The The Mortal War concluded with a three-way battle between the Vaults, the Depths, and Aldric's Wyrthkin, ending in Aldric's self-sacrifice.

In the aftermath of the battle, a peace was brokered between the Vaults and the Depths. The memories of the Wyrthkin would be erased, and humanity would be free to chart its own destiny, be it toward the Light or Darkness. The The Sovereign Evils however, had no intention of honoring the truce, and would continue their efforts to corrupt humanity.

#### Freedom Imperfect

The The Gilded Age was a short-lived golden age for Vaerath. One that ended in the The Spellbinder Wars, which ushered in the The Covenant Age. Notably, the events of the conflict 'stained' the fabric of Vaerath, making it easier for demons to manifest within the mortal realm. The The Covenant Age would see the formation of Akarum, though the teachings of Orath flourished in obscurity for the next millennium.

The The Great Banishment likewise changed the fortunes of Vaerath, and not for the better. In the time since the The Mortal War, the The Sovereign Evils had continued to focus on subverting humanity to their side, while the The Minor Evils believed that the Primes were afraid to continue the war with the Vaults. The result was a civil war in the Depths, which saw the The Sovereign Evils banished to Vaerath. This led to Valdrus creating the Valorim, which spent over a decade hunting down and imprisoning the three Primes. Before their capture, the Primes had spread terror, hatred, and destruction throughout humanity for years, and while all three were eventually imprisoned, this was a temporary victory, for Sorvax, Veldris, and Karruth would all come to corrupt their jailers in time. Furthermore, like with the The Spellbinder Wars, the events of the The Great Banishment negatively affected the very nature of the mortal realm. Unnatural and evil forced influenced most, if not all, of Vaerath's native creatures to some degree.

Following the sealing of the Primes, the Akarum faith emerged into prominence. While the religion had been founded a millennium before, the conversion of Emperor Valdran of Kaeldris to the faith, and subsequent election of the first High-Covenant, Akarum was formally codified. The subsequent Akarum Crusade spread the faith to the far west, ensuring that few reigions of Vaerath were untouched by the faith. However, two centuries later, this was followed by the Akarum Inquisition, which led to the suppression of other faiths and schisms within the church.

#### Return of the Primes

The 1260s saw a series of cataclysmic events rock the mortal realm. By this point, Akarum had been corrupted by Veldris, who sent his servant, Maldus, to awake Sorvax in Ashveil. This led to the Darkening of Ashveil, which saw the deaths of King Aldrath and Queen Asylla, the abduction of their son, Albrecht, and general calamity befalling the town. While Sorvax was ultimately defeated by a trio of heroes, this was naught but a pyrrhic victory. Sorvax corrupted Aidan (the hero who had slain him), who, under Sorvax's thrall, journeyed eastward in the guise of the Shadow Wanderer.

Under the guidance of Erdan Mourne, a new group of heroes pursued the Wanderer, but were always one step behind. Karruth was freed from his prison in Sandmere, and in Korrath, the three Primes were reunited for the first time in centuries. The heroes were forced to fight their way through Korrath, slay the members of the Akarum High Council, and in so doing, topple Vaerath's largest faith. Ultimately, Veldris was slain in Korrath, and Sorvax in the Depths itself. Karruth, however, remained at large, and mustered an army to march on Mount Valdur itself.

Despite the efforts of the Mountainborn, Stolvern fell to Karruth's army, and Thornholt came under siege. The treachery of Korvel allowed Karruth to enter Mount Valdur itself, and while the Sovereign of Ruin was slain by the heroes, this was not before Karruth had corrupted the The Vaultstone. If nothing was done, Karruth's destructive energies would corrupt all of humanity. Thus, Valdrus used Lumindral to shatter the The Vaultstone. The resulting explosion devastated the The Northern Marches, turning it into the hellish, toxic landscape that would become known as the The Blightlands.

#### Age of Unraveling

"A nightmare come to life. Vaerath's unmaking is nearly upon us. As the armies of the Depths breach our world, the Sovereign of Dread torments us with visions, crushing our spirits before corrupting our souls. Steel yourself. Do not let him shatter your resolve. Though Sorvax assaults our minds with images of power pouring from his domain, he has also exposed the power hidden within. If there is any hope for Vaerath at all, its fortunes are held in the hands of the fearless."

After the destruction of the The Vaultstone, blight began to overrun the world's oceans and forests, and a wasting sickness began to spread through its cities. Where once the The Vaultstone had hid Vaerath from the Vaults and the Depths, now the world was open to the eyes of angels and demons, and both stared intently at the mortal realm. The essence of Vaerath itself and every person on it began to change. By this stage, human civilization was fragmented, with only a few functioning cities left on Vaerath.


c. 1273, Sorvax's growing influence warped sections of Vaerath into nightmarish hellscapes. Foreboding creept across the world as thousands found their dreams plagued by unexplained nightmares. The dread of the afflicted gave way to madness. Tales of self-mutilation and depraved crimes spread like silent fire. In shadowed places across the world, the air was rent as hellish maws burst out of the world's topsoil. As Sorvax's emergence drew nigh, demons paved the way for his manifestation, running rampant across the mortal realm.

#### The Twilight of Creation

After twenty years of relative calm, calamity once again befell Vaerath. Mordecai and Voran, who had been enemies since the The Great Banishment, both shared the same goal of subjugating humanity into their fold. Voran came to impersonate Theron II of Kaeldris, and Valdrus, having forsaken his immortality to aid humanity, descended to the mortal realm as the The Cast-Down Star. There, he allied with a group of heroes, including the Vergalem and Seyna, the ward of Erdan Mourne.

The heroes travelled to Aeldrum where they allied with Morwen. She helped them uncover the location of the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, which could trap multiple essences of both angels and demons, and permanently destroy them. The Obsidian Soulcrystal in hand, they confronted Voran and revealed his great deception. Defeating him, they sealed him inside the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, leaving Mordecai as the last Great Evil to be imprisoned.

They did not need to wait long, as Mordecai launched an invasion of Vaerath, besieging the fortress of Ironhold. After a titanic battle, Mordecai was defeated, and in turn imprisoned inside the The Obsidian Soulcrystal. However, it was at this point that Morwen revealed her true colors as a servant of Sorvax. Enacting a plan over 20 years in the making, Seyna served as Sorvax's host, leading the Sovereign of Dread to be reborn as a singular Sovereign Evil, incorporating all the essences of the The Greater Evils into a singular being.

Ultimately, Sorvax was defeated, but peace was not to last, as months later, Vaerath was assaulted by the Deathbringers; a force of undead thralls and angels in service to Nocthar. With the The Greater Evils defeated, Nocthar reasoned that humanity was the last "demonic" force left in Creation, and thus, had to be eliminated.

The Deathbringers assaulted numerous cities and populations. Making matters worse, Nocthar took the The Obsidian Soulcrystal for himself and used it to tear out the abyssal essence of humanity across Vaerath. Nocthar was confronted by the Vergalem inside the The Chaos Citadel, and in the ensuing battle, Nocthar destroyed the The Obsidian Soulcrystal to take the essences of the The Greater Evils into himself. Even this was not enough to stop the Vergalem however, and the Arbiter of Death was defeated. However, not only was Sorvax freed by Nocthar's death, but by this point, 50% (or even 90%) of the human race having been killed. Misery followed for mankind as religious schisms, war, and famine ravaged Vaerath.

#### The The Age of Ruin

After the defeat of Nocthar, monsters became an issue all over Vaerath. Scouts regularly reported to Valdrus on these events, so he could best send the Vergalem to where they were most needed. As time wore on, Valdrus began to realize that the tasks of the Vergalem were never-ending.

Over the next five decades, Vaerath became a bleak and shattered hellscape, bereft of hope and beset by demons. Chaos threatened to consume Vaerath as evil and impiety continued to spread. Depraved demons and wildlife roamed the land unchecked, as terrified townsfolk huddled in dark corners, trying to survive one more day. This period of time became known as "the The Age of Ruin."

Demons gained control over areas of Vaerath, the most afflicted being those subsumed by helltides. Voran spread his falsehoods to make the mortal realm ever more fractious.

In light of the power vacuum Nocthar's actions had created, some rose up to attempt to fill the void, including the Akarum church and a resurgent Temple of the The Three-Faced. By the end of the The Age of Ruin, organized human civilization had largely collapsed across the world.

#### The Age of Malice

With the return of Morraia to Vaerath, more demons rose from the Depths to blight the mortal realm, and a new wave of cultists and worshippers were spawned, but regardless of one's faith, all prayers remained unanswered. Morraia herself began a brutal campaign to conquer the now ruined world. The The Fields of Malice formed as a result of her presence.

After Morraia's death, demons from the darkest reaches of the Depths manifested within Vaerath, ushering in a new era of death and wanton destruction. Endless waves of demons threatened to overwhelm Vaerath's meager defenses.
