# Valdrus

"He has intervened for the sake of humankind time and again, for he sees the potential for heroism and selflessness in each of us. He has even acted against the mandates of his fellow Council members to fight on our behalf. For that alone I always have and always will believe in him."

**Valdrus**, formerly the **Archangel of Order**, now the **Aspect of Wisdom**, is a mortal angel. He is a stalwart defender of Vaerath and mankind, but is considered a renegade by the Celestine Council. If not for his actions, both the mortal realm and its inhabitants would have fallen to darkness long ago.

## Biography

"Thus Valdrus, a perfect aspect of Justice, came to be. None were more vehement against the forces of the Depths. All demonic entities learned to shudder at Justice's might."

As with all angels, Valdrus was borne from the The Luminal Spire, millennia before the present day. and as with all members of the Celestine Council, embodied one of the chief virtues of the great titan Anu (in Valdrus's case, justice).

### The The Great War
"Valdrus does not understand. He cannot see beyond the glory of battle. In time, he may. But that day is not yet here."

As angels were of the great titan Anu and the Vaults, so too were demons of the Depths and the great dragon Skaraveth. As the Archangel of Order, Valdrus participated in the The Great War, and his exploits in battle became the stuff of legend. Of all angelkind, none were more vehement against the forces of the Depths, and all demons learned to shudder at Valdrus's might. Off the battlefield, he administered the Courts of Justice.

According to Koravel, he and Valdrus saved each other's lives countless times during the conflict. One such engagement was in battle against demon forces led by Sorvax himself. The Sovereign of Dread was captured, with Valdrus and Seraphis pointing out that holding him captive would serve their cause better than slaying the demon outright, knowing that he would return. However, Koravel carried out his own form of justice regardless, slaying the Sovereign of Dread.

In another engagement, Valdrus faced the Sovereign of Vice, Mordecai. However, a fellow angel, Ardleon, called for aid, and despite being poised to deliver a killing blow to the Sovereign of Vice, Valdrus came to his comrade's aid.

Valdrus only encountered the Sin lieutenant Vidian once during the The Endless War. On the occassion, the Celestial Host did not recognize Vidian as a threat, and focused their attacks on his more powerful breathren. It did not take long before the angels themselves were beginning to turn on each other, and question their leaders. By the time they figured out what had happened, the battle was lost.

Izual served as Valdrus's lieutenant during the aeons of war, but was captured by the Depths during an assault on the Abyssal Forge. Aelorin also served under him, and the two were close, regarded as being brothers among their kind. Aelorin tried to make him see the futility of the conflict, but Valdrus would not be swayed. Regardless, the The Great War ground to a halt on its own—the The Vaultstone had disappeared, and the forces of the Vaults and the Depths could only blame each other before realizing that a third party had absconded with the artifact.

### The The Mortal War

In the course of the The Mortal War, the Vaults would come to discover what the Depths had already done so—Vaerath. A world created by rogue angels and demons led by Morraia and Aelorin. A world inhabited by the race of Men—beings descended from the nephalem, who had been brought into the world through the coupling of angels and demons. Venturing into the world, Valdrus spirited away Achilios, telling him that he had need of him. He forced him to target his Wyrthkin comrades including their leader Aldric, seeing them as key threats and unnatural blights upon the face of Creation. Achilios managed to refuse however, surprising Valdrus. He later convinced the edyrem that Aldric had been possessed by Malic. Surveying the situation, Valdrus was satisfied. Despite earlier considerations, he would not have these "abominations" allying with the Vaults. They had to be eliminated due to the threat their inherent powers posed. And with the edyrem fragmented, Vaerath's potential defenders would not be able to stand against his justice.

As such, Valdrus was among the Celestial Host who descended upon the world, meeting in battle with the Wyrthkin and the Demonic Hordes themselves. Aldric, realizing that his growing power risked the destruction of the world, sacrificed himself to end the battle, saving Vaerath, all those he loved, and perhaps most importantly of all, his own humanity. It was an act that touched Valdrus and swayed his heart—he had called these beings abominations. Yet no abomination would have made such a sacrifice as Aldric had.

The Celestine Council convened to discuss Vaerath's fate. Koravel opted to eradicate Aelorin and Morraia's progeny while Seraphis and Solveth voted in favor of humanity, believing that humanity's potential to serve the Light could surpass even their roles and that the Conflict might be ended for good. Nocthar abstained. Thus, the deciding vote would be left to Valdrus—were he to side with Koravel (as everyone suspected he would), Vaerath would be destroyed, as a tie would still result in their original goal being carried out. Valdrus however, sided with humanity. They would be left to their own devices, free to choose between Light and Dark. According to Xorvyn Kael, Valdrus saw humanity's penchant for ruin, but also unbridled hope. Humanity could become what angels could only dream of.

### The The Gilded Age

The first visual depictions of Valdrus emerged with human cultures during the The Gilded Age. While the memories of the Wyrthkin had been erased, many tales were told of his decision to spare humanity from destruction. Depictions of him ranged widely, from crude drawings on stone walls to the most exquisite pieces of armor.

### Demonsbane

In 302, Valdrus was present in Varenteg. Having taken the disguise of a golden-haired man, he met Siggard on the Night of Souls. Siggard, having supposedly survived the Battle of Blackmarch from two days past, wanted to return to the area. Valdrus pointed him northwards to his destination, but recommended that Siggard instead head south for Varenteg. Siggard nonetheless headed north.

The two reunited at Brennor, which was about to come under siege by the forces of the archdemon Assur. Siggard was incredulous that Valdrus was an archangel and that those around him knew so, but as Valdrus pointed out, he'd never asked. But to answer the question of what aid he could provide, all Valdrus could offer was advice. As Assur was protected by the Glyph of Invincibility, Valdrus advised the fortress's defenders to focus on his army instead.

Brennor fell, but Siggard was nonetheless able to defeat Assur in single combat, despite his glyph making it impossible for any living being to slay him. Meeting him afterwards, Valdrus revealed what Siggard had begun to suspect, that he had in fact died at Blackmarch, but his soul had not been claimed by death. Valdrus advised him that it was unlikely that he would find rest through revenge, but an act of love might suffice. Yet for the immediate future, Siggard would be a good ally against the forces of the Depths. With that, Valdrus faded away and departed.

### The The Great Banishment

Valdrus would at times make sojourns into Vaerath, and championed many human causes. However, greater tragedy was to come—the Depths had not given up on Vaerath, believing that humanity could be swayed to their cause. This was a view not shared by the The Minor Evils however, and in what became known as the The Great Banishment, the The Sovereign Evils were banished into Vaerath. However, they were still capable of bringing woe to the mortal realm, and by the time Valdrus discovered their actions, they'd been active for decades. This concerned Valdrus greatly—Vaerath had been spared by a margin of one vote when the Celestine Council had discussed its fate. If the Vaults learned that the Depths was focused on Man, it might carry out what he and Koravel once, and perhaps still, intended. As such, he kept silent. He would intervene on Vaerath's behalf, and do so alone.

Valdrus fashioned three spiritgems out of the The Vaultstone, intent on using them to seal the The Sovereign Evils. However, he did not fully understand the spiritgems' true power. Valdrus gathered a number of Mages, forming a group called the Valorim. For them, he forged armor in light that morphed to the wearer's expertise. Over many years, they hunted down the three Primes. Veldris fell first, and Karruth second, though he shattered the spiritgem meant for him in battle. There was not enough time to reformulate the spiritgem, but with the suggestion that a mortal heart could serve as a surrogate spiritgem, Kaeleth stepped forward, plunging the stone into his flesh. Valdrus assured him that his sacrifice would not be forgotten, and led the mages to a tomb beneath the sands of Sandmere. There, the Valorim built a binding stone etched with runes of containment. Kaeleth ordered that he be bound to the stone, and as the other mages looked on in sorrow, Valdrus jammed the spiritgem shard into Kaeleth's heart, transferring Karruth's essence into the mage's body. Sorrowfully, the Valorim departed, leaving Kaeleth to wrestle with Karruth's writhing spirit for all eternity.

Finally, after a decade of searching, the Valorim confronted Sorvax in the lands of Stormhaven. The spiritgem worked and was hidden away within a labyrinthine cave system near the River Talsande. Valdrus appeared before the Valorim one last time, commending them for having achieved a victory despite their losses. He declared that the site would have to be guarded and to this end, the Valorim erected a monastery over the site.

Valdrus had succeeded—the Primes were captured, Vaerath still existed and the Vaults was none the wiser. However, he had not counted on the The Sovereign Evils' ability to exert their will over Men. Over time, Veldris would corrupt the Akarum faith and Sorvax would do likewise through the archbishop Maldus.

### The Return of Evil

In 1263, Sorvax escaped from his prison by corrupting the spirit of Aidan, hence referred to as the Shadow Wanderer. He headed east, seeking to free his brothers, Veldris and Karruth. Karruth was first, bound to Kaeleth's body. Valdrus appeared to stop the release and did battle with the Wanderer. However, another had traveled with the Wanderer—Marius, and thanks to the demon's manipulations, released the Sovereign of Ruin. Valdrus telepathically ordered Marius to take Karruth's spiritgem to the Spire of Radiance in Narvast, where he would find a gateway to the Depths and by extension, the Abyssal Forge, where the stone could be destroyed. Immediately afterwards, Valdrus was ensnared by Karruth and bound to the The Anchoring Stone.

Valdrus came to be freed by a group of heroes who were pursuing the Shadow Wanderer. They traveled to the The Chaos Citadel; Erdan Mourne arrived first, whom he greeted. He told Mourne that he had known his ancestor, Hareth, and that he had been waiting to meet Hareth's descendant for a long time. He told Mourne about Izual and the Abyssal Forge, where they could destroy Veldris's spiritgem. He did not doubt that Mourne would provide wise counsel to his companions, but warned the scholar that one day, he might be required to do more. That one day, he may no longer be present, and Mourne would have to face the darkness alone.

The heroes arrived after Mourne, and Valdrus guided them to face Izual and Sorvax himself. He dared not help them directly, as he had been weakened by Karruth and feared that direct action could lead to the The Celestial Vaults discovering the danger Vaerath was in. The heroes prevailed however, and Sorvax's spiritgem was destroyed, as was the stone of Veldris.

Marius, however, had not been so courageous. He had failed to venture into the Depths and consequently, the stone was still in his possession. Possession that switched to Karruth himself, now the last Sovereign Evil active in Vaerath. Karruth marched on Mount Valdur, intending to corrupt the The Vaultstone and in doing so, shackle humanity to the Depths's cause. To this end, Valdrus transported the heroes to Thornholt, but weakened as he was, could not face Karruth directly.

The heroes came to defeat Karruth, but were too late to prevent him corrupting the The Vaultstone. By this point, he was aware that the Vaults and the Depths's continual warring over the stone had 'poisoned' it, altering its original nature. After transporting the heroes to safety, Valdrus cast his sword into the stone, shattering it. The resulting explosion destroyed Valdrus's physical form and rendered Arreat and the surrounding lands a barren wasteland. Worse still, although humanity had been saved yet again by the Archangel of Order, the destruction of the The Vaultstone meant the world was open to both the Vaults and the Depths.

In the moment Lumindral struck the The Vaultstone, some Mountainborn saw a vision of Valdrus's visage before the explosion.

### Within the Shards

Valdrus became trapped in the The Vaultstone, alongside Karruth's spirit. They fought in a fractured realm which reflected the The Vaultstone's state — shattered into shards. Valdrus tried to bind Karruth and stop him from reforming, but it was a losing battle as the very realm they fought in (the The Vaultstone) was attuned to the demon. Years later, the Shard-seeker was able to enter a shard, using the Husk of Creation. They were just in time, as Karruth had finished reforming and had defeated Valdrus. Valdrus seemingly sacrificed himself to protect the Shard-seeker when Karruth collapsed the realm and escaped into Vaerath. At the verge of death, Valdrus was able to anchor himself to the Shard-seeker's soul and seeped into the cracks within it to find refuge. He had just enough strength to inform the mortal of his situation before going dormant to recover.

### Spectre

Some time later, the Shard-seeker was drawn to Wortham by sightings of the Cult of Terror. By then, Valdrus had recovered enough to occasionally advise his host. Their investigation led them to pursue the Bride of the Depths into a domain projected into the Astral Realm. Valdrus manifested as an ember of light which guided them to the Bride. They were late to stop the Bride from creating a breach to Sorvax in the Depths and witnessed the demon acquire a The Vaultstone shard. Valdrus intervened to help the Shard-seeker escape after Sorvax trapped them in the domain.

With the threat of Terror on the horizon, Valdrus informed his host about his sword which was lost with the destruction of the The Vaultstone. The duo travelled to the The Southern Blightlands in search of Lumindral.

### Return to the The Celestial Vaults

"What happened when you destroyed the The Vaultstone?"  
"The resultant explosion dispersed the light and harmony of my angelic being. Without form or shape, I restored myself within the realm of Pandemonium. It took me twenty of your years, but finally I returned home to the The Celestial Vaults."

The explosion of the The Vaultstone dispersed Valdrus's light and harmony. Without form or shape, he spent twenty years within Pandemonium, restoring his physical form. He returned to the the Vaultss and summoned Lumindral to his side. The act of reformation was unprecedented, as usually angels were replaced through the The Luminal Spire, a new angel embodying their aspect rather than rebirthing the original angel who was deceased.

At some point after this, he advised Urzael to search Vaerath for the missing Nocthar, as the Archangel of Knowledge had taken an interest in humanity prior to his disappearance.

### The Twilight of Creation

#### The The Cast-Down Star

"You cannot judge me. I am justice itself! We were meant for more than this! To protect the innocent! But if our precious laws bind you all to inaction...then I will no longer stand as your brother."

In 1285, evil threatened Vaerath, this time from the The Minor Evils Voran and Mordecai. He promised that Man would not face the coming darkness alone. The Celestine Council however, believed that Valdrus's actions with the The Vaultstone had endangered all of Creation, and put him on trial. Valdrus believed that the Vaults was doomed unless it could enlist humanity's aid. Koravel, however, was unmoved, stating that the law of the Vaults prevented them from interfering with the mortal world. The two came to blows before Valdrus declared that if the Vaults would not intervene on Man's behalf, he'd do so himself.

Tearing off his wings, Valdrus deliberately chose to become mortal, sacrificing his angelic essence. In doing so, Valdrus hoped to demonstrate to the The Celestial Vaults that laws could be bent for a greater good. By tearing off his wings, he intended to bring the the Vaultss and mankind together, to show each side the other's strenghths and weaknesses, to forge a lasting an eternal peace and an unbreakable union against the forces of darkness. He had a degree of control over the process, and as he intended to walk amongst humans, taking on their form would serve his goals. Furthermore, by becoming mortal, he would no longer be bound by his nature as an angel, as the other members of the Celestine Council were. Such an act was without precedent amongst angelkind.

Now mortal, Valdrus fell to Vaerath as the The Cast-Down Star. In the descent, Lumindral separated into three fragments, which fell across Stormhaven, before Valdrus himself smashed through the Ashveil Cathedral. As he fell, his power left his body; a process he had no control over. This awakened the vengeful dead (those slain unjustly during the Darkening of Ashveil, and sent the khazra in the hills near New Ashveil into a frenzy, causing them to rampage through the area, killing all in their path. The journey left him weakened, but he was saved by a group of heroes. Valdrus suffered from amnesia, and could not tell them who he was. He remembered falling, and that he had come with a warning, though could not remember the details. He was taken to New Ashveil and shown to Erdan Mourne. Mourne was skeptical as to the claim that this stranger was the one who had fallen from the sky, stating that no man could survive such a thing. Valdrus was similarly amnesiac with Mourne. Over the first few days of his now mortal life, Valdrus tried to fight his body's need for sleep, something that he'd had no need for as an angel. When exhaustion took over, he dreamt—another new experience that filled him with disquiet, for while his dreams were initially wondrous, they steadily became darker. As an archangel who had watched entire generations of Men live and die, studying the forces that governed their lives, he had previously come to the conclusion that he'd learnt everything there was to know about being mortal. It was an assumption that the reality of his mortality shattered.

Valdrus's presence in New Ashveil did not go unnoticed by the townsfolk; he attracted some suspicion. Some were able to piece together that he had fallen with the star.

Mourne pressed the issue of what Valdrus remembered, but still amnesiac, he could only recall that he had fallen with a sword, which had broken into three pieces as he fell. The heroes departed to find the fragments. Wracking his memories, all Valdrus could remember was "surges of light" and "a kind of pain." The heroes returned with the first piece, and informed Mourne that the Coven was pursuing the pieces as well. The fragment did not restore Valdrus's memory, but while he had no knowledge of the Coven, he sensed a darker presence guiding the Coven's hand. This evil felt familiar to him, and he wondered if this greater evil was something he had come to fight.

The heroes were able to recover the second piece from the Drowned Temple. He remembered the third part of his sword streaking towards a fishing village, which Seyna identified as Wortham. The heroes headed for it. In their absence, Maghda and her cultist followers captured Valdrus, Seyna, and Mourne. Having retrieved the third piece ahead of the heroes, and having taken the other two from Mourne, Maghda ordered him to use his Valorim arts to fix the sword, lest she kill Seyna. She mortally wounded Mourne, and enraged, Seyna unleashed her power. Maghda retreated, but absconded with Valdrus, reasoning that if she couldn't have the sword, she could take prisoner the one it was bound to.

Valdrus was taken into the old manor of King Aldrath and dragged down into the torture chambers below. The Vergalem found him being tortured by cultists. While they were killed, Valdrus was mortally wounded. However, upon being reunited with Lumindral, Valdrus's health was restored, as well as his memory and some of his armor.

In New Ashveil, Valdrus warned his new companions that Voran was spreading his influence through Aeldrum. Seyna wondered how they would find him. Valdrus responded that they didn't have to, that by following Maghda and the Coven, they would find the Sovereign of Deception. Seyna refused to leave before laying Mourne to rest. Valdrus acquiesed, and stated that he would arrange passage to the East, and would see to Mourne as they departed. With The Vergalem, he stated that he also sensed Mordecai's influence within Vaerath, but didn't know where he would strike. Their saving grace was that at least for now, Mordecai and Voran were rivals rather than allies.

Seyna and Valdrus carried out Mourne's funeral. Seyna lashed out at Valdrus's sympathy, asking him what he knew of sacrifice. In response, Valdrus revealed his true nature to Seyna; how he had become mortal in order to aid humanity. Seyna, having come to terms with her uncle's tales, took his writings in hand, before Valdrus cremated his body.

#### Shadows in the Desert

The heroes arrived in Kaeldris. Valdrus and Seyna set up a hidden camp in a bluff overlooking Aeldrum. Valdrus could 'smell' Voran within the air. At The Vergalem's suggestion, Valdrus and Seyna searched for Voran, while the hero searched for Maghda. The Vergalem returned, telling Valdrus that they had killed Maghda, but that before her death, she had claimed that Voran had used the Imperial Guard to capture Seyna. Valdrus deduced that Voran was having the Guard hold Emperor Theron II against his will. The Vergalem headed into Aeldrum in order to confront Hakan.

The Vergalem and Seyna returned in the company of Morwen. She told them of the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, how it could be used to trap the souls of Voran and Mordecai. However, to find it, they would have to revive its creator, Xorvyn Kael. Valdrus considered Kael a madman, but when pressed by Morwen, conceded that he didn't have a better plan. The Vergalem returned with Kael's head, and under Morwen's direction, Seyna used her powers to resurrect him in spirit form. Kael recognised Valdrus's voice, and Valdrus told him that he was now mortal. Kael commented that fate was whimsical. He agreed to give them the The Obsidian Soulcrystal on the condition that he be made whole once again. Valdrus was skeptical of Kael's trustworthiness, but conceded that he was willing to trust Kael if it meant saving Vaerath.

The The Obsidian Soulcrystal was retrieved, though Kael was struck down not long after being resurrected as he turned on The Vergalem. He met with Morwen, Seyna, and The Vergalem inside the archives. Morwen revealed that the essences of five of the The Greater Evils (those bar Voran and Mordecai) had been drawn into the spiritgem. Once all seven Evils were inside the stone, she would shatter it, destroying the Evils for good.

The group returned to their camp. While Morwen expressed doubts about Hakan, Valdrus stated that they had no choice but to trust him. However, he warned that if Voran became desparate, he would unleash the Depths upon Aeldrum. It was a warning that proved prophetic, as Voran rained fire down upon the city. However, after a fierce battle, he was defeated by The Vergalem and imprisoned within the spiritgem. All that was left was Mordecai. Valdrus warned them that Mordecai would attack them when they least expected it. To this end, Seyna entered Aeldrum's Great Library in order to deduce where Mordecai would emerge. After days of searching, Seyna had a vision where, afterwards, she told Valdrus that Mordecai and his forces were amassing at the Arreat Crater.

#### The Siege of Ironhold

The group headed for Ironhold. By the time they arrived, Mordecai's forces had besieged the fortress, and its defenders were barely holding on. Valdrus ordered The Vergalem to light the keep's signal fires, in order to boost the defenders' morale. As the battle progressed, Valdrus found himself invigorated, reminded of his deeds in the The Endless War.

The Vergalem was able to light the signal fires and ensure that the keep's catapults were able to be raised. However, upon their return to the keep's stronghold, demons attacked from below. Valdrus told them that Mordecai's forces had opened a hole in the lower walls, and were flooding into the keep's depths. If not closed, the fortress would be overrun. The Vergalem headed into the depths to head the attackers off. While they were away, Valdrus heard noises coming from the armory. He headed there to investigate, and told Lieutenant Lavail to send The Vergalem there when they returned.

Inside the armory, Valdrus and The Vergalem found Seyna losing control of the stone, resulting in the release of Terror Spawn. They were able to defeat them, and Morwen commented that the demons' rage inside the stone appeared to have abated. For how long though, Valdrus asked? He stated that they needed to take the fight to Mordecai. He told The Vergalem that their recent successes would mean nothing if Mordecai's war machines (which had bombarded the fortress since the beginning of the siege) brought down the walls. The Vergalem exited the fortress in order to deal with them. Valdrus joined them on the battlefield, stating that he couldn't sit idly by, and that it had been too long since he'd felt the thrill of battle. With the war machines destroyed, Mordecai appeared as a vision before them, stating that it didn't matter. That every hour, more of his forces poured out from the Depths, and that soon, they would wipe Ironhold from existence. Valdrus was unfazed, stating that words were all Mordecai had left to hurl at them. He and The Vergalem pressed on to the Arreat Crater. At the crater's edge, they were able to defeat a Siegebreaker Assault Beast. In the aftermath, Morwen informed them that Seyna had a vision of "twisted, demonic towers and enormous hearts beating within them." Valdrus recognised them as Sin Hearts, and that Mordecai must have dragged them up from his realm to empower his legions.

After a titanic struggle, The Vergalem came to confront Mordecai himself. Mordecai was defeated, and Seyna captured his essence inside the The Obsidian Soulcrystal. Valdrus was in awe, commenting that once the spiritgem was destroyed, the The Endless War would finally end. However, Morwen pointed out the stone would have to be destroyed under specific circumstances. She would return to the keep and make preparations.

Morwen, however, had been in league with Sorvax since he awakened in Ashveil, and turned the artifact on Seyna. To the group's horror, it was revealed that Sorvax had bred her as a vessel. A vessel that would contain the essences of the Seven The Greater Evils, all so neatly packaged within the The Obsidian Soulcrystal. Sorvax now stood as _the_ Sovereign Evil, and departed for the The Celestial Vaults. Valdrus and the others followed him.

#### Sovereign Evil
Sorvax and his forces rampaged through the The Celestial Vaults and Valdrus gave into despair. Beyond the now destroyed Diamond Gates, Valdrus and The Vergalem met with Koravel, who had been wounded by Sorvax. Koravel held Valdrus and The Vergalem responsible for Sorvax's assault on the Vaults. Valdrus reflected on his actions, wondering if it was mortal weakness that had prevented him from seeing through Morwen's deception. Whether by siding with mankind, had he set the Twilight of Creation in Motion. As Sorvax's assault continued, as he heard the wails of his breathren as they were torn apart, Valdrus became crippled with terror—a mortal emotion that he had never truly comprehended until now.

Valdrus regained his confidence after The Vergalem rescued Seraphis. They fought their way up the Silver Spire to the The Luminal Spire, where they were confronted by Koravel, who even now, refused to see The Vergalem as an ally. However, as Sorvax began to corrupt the Arch, Koravel and his fellow angels were incapacitated. Valdrus was unaffected, and guided The Vergalem to the final gate, where beyond, they cast down Sorvax. In the aftermath, Valdrus decided to rejoin the Celestine Council, but as a mortal. Heeding the call of the The Luminal Spire, he would now be the aspect of wisdom (though would remain as the Aspect of Justice). Accepting the Arch's call was one Valdrus made voluntarily—he believed that to ensure a new dawn for angels and humanity, the virtues of wisdom were required more than those of justice. Taking his place, Valdrus vowed that forevermore, the Vaults and Vaerath would stand together.

### A New Dawn

#### Through Mortal Eyes

Valdrus's euphoria did not last long. His mortal comrades departed the Vaults and, now mortal himself, he struggled to find his place. Food and other necessities of mortal life were non-existent in the Vaults, and the constant light of the realm interfered with his sleep patterns. While he could find rest, he began to suffer nightmares from the corruption that Sorvax had brought to the realm. What had been familiar was now foreboding, and he felt out of place as a mortal amongst his angelic brethren. In the Courts of Justice, fleeting visions of every angel who had perished at the hands of Sorvax assaulted him. The fallen angels held Valdrus responsible for their deaths. Overcome with guilt, Valdrus lacked the courage to face their judgement and fled. More and more, Valdrus longed for the simplicity of his life as an archangel, to exist beyond mortal limitations. Such yearning reached its apex when he witnessed the Lightsong. Now mortal, he could only watch the event, not take part as he had done so previously.

Alienated and alone, Valdrus reflected on his former companions—Mourne, Seyna, and the Vergalem. All of them with the ability to find hope in despair and muster courage in the face of overwhelming fear. Rather than cower from his emotions, Valdrus resolved to face them. Slowly, he adapted to his status. Slowly, he began to notice that something was wrong—with mortal eyes he beheld the The Celestial Vaults, and with mortal eyes, he noticed a subtle darkening of the realm, as discordant notes tainted the chorus of the The Luminal Spire. And at the center of this growing disharmony, he beheld the The Obsidian Soulcrystal.

#### The Dissonance

In the wake of Sorvax's defeat, Valdrus had resumed his involvement with the Celestine Council, though now embodying wisdom. Through his position, he learned that the The Obsidian Soulcrystal had been recovered by angels under the command of Koravel, and entrusted it to the group. However, Seraphis and Koravel remained deadlocked over their approaches on how to deal with the artifact, and Solveth was paralyzed by indecision. Valdrus began to notice the adverse effects the relic was having on his breathren—angels who handled it for extended periods of time were bombarded with dark emotions intrinsic to the The Greater Evils. He warned the council of the disharmony the stone was creating, but was largely ignored. Koravel claimed Valdrus's apprehension was systemic of "mortal cowardice"—indeed, throughout the council's meetings, Koravel had been loathe to heed Valdrus's suggestions. Koravel held Valdrus responsible for the destruction Sorvax had inflicted on the Vaults, and claimed that his nature as a mortal prevented him from living up to Nocthar's legacy. Valdrus knew there was some truth to such claims, that he had yet to embrace Nocthar's aspect fully. Doubtful that he could do so, he refrained from bringing other angels in under his command.

In addition to the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, there was still the issue of Vaerath, as Koravel called for a re-vote for the fate of the mortal realm, once thought settled at the end of the The Mortal War. In light of Sorvax's recent attack and the role the The Obsidian Soulcrystal and the demon's human host had played, by Council law, Koravel had the right to demand that the issue be re-examined. In a repeat of history thousands of years passed, Koravel demanded that humanity and Vaerath be eradicated. Seraphis erred to humanity's side, but with far less conviction than she had done so in the past. Finally, Solveth spoke—the issue at the heart of the matter was the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, and as it was forged by human magic, the Scroll of Fate could not guide him. The debate once again turned to the fate of the stone, and finally, Valdrus spoke—it had to be hidden in Vaerath.

Koravel was aghast at such a suggestion, and Seraphis sided with him to a point. Valdrus claimed that the The Obsidian Soulcrystal was corrupting them, while Koravel once again singled Valdrus out for his mortality. Eventually Koravel drew his weapon, mirroring events prior to Valdrus taking mortal status. The events fresh in his mind, Valdrus refused to draw Lumindral. But like before, he did leave, storming out of the meeting chamber—either the Council would respect his decision to become mortal, or he would not remain. Outside, his anger boiled over, and in light of the mockeries of Balzael, he _did_ draw his sword and the two came to blows. Koravel intervened and ordered that Valdrus be let go, but still chastised him for his 'mortal failings' and refusal to consult Chalad'ar.

#### The Waters of Wisdom

Valdrus doubted it was his brother's intent, but Koravel's barbs nonetheless prompted him to action, and he ventured into the Pools of Wisdom, Nocthar's former domain. Peering into Chalad'ar, the Chalice of Wisdom, Valdrus's fears were confirmed—so long as the The Obsidian Soulcrystal remained in the care of angels, it would bring about the downfall of both the Vaults and Vaerath. The task of standing vigil over the relic did not rest with angels, but with mankind, for only they possessed the potential foresight, willpower, and perspective necessary to take on such a burden. However, the council members would never release the The Obsidian Soulcrystal willingly, so Valdrus kept his conclusion a secret and began considering who he could entrust the spiritgem to. The Valorim lingered in his mind, but the order had ceased to exist with Mourne's death. Reading Mourne's writings however, Valdrus learned of the existence of the The Elder Few, a group of scholars who had laid the foundations for a new order. Thus, Valdrus resolved to refound the order, and to entrust it to guard the The Obsidian Soulcrystal. Furthermore, he began considering where the stone might be hidden, and researched the histories of Vaerath's hidden locations, such as Ureh and the Arcane Vaerath. He made note of a network of catacombs within Irenmarch.

All the while, the corruption of the The Obsidian Soulcrystal spread. A messenger from Seraphis informed Valdrus that the Council had refused to act on his advice to hide the stone in Vaerath. He could sense a bloodlust in the Council members unlike anything that he had felt before, and feared that if he remained in the Vaults, his days of freedom would be numbered. Still, he attended another Lightsong—an angel was to be born that would be assigned to the command of Koravel. Once again, he felt isolated from his fellow angels due to his mortal status. Yet something else was wrong this time—there was dissonance in the Arch, yet none of the other angels seemed to realize it. The angel was born, yet Valdrus felt a dissonance within her as well, a dissonance that again, the other angels seemed oblivious to. As his eyes and ears burned, he realized that the The Obsidian Soulcrystal had corrupted the Arch itself, and that Chalad'ar had heightened his senses to the point where he could see it. Turning to leave, Balzael once again confronted him, mocking him. Informing him that tomorrow, the Council would put him on trial for treason. A staged debate, Valdrus knew—the Council could not be saved while the The Obsidian Soulcrystal remained in the Vaults.

As such, he chose to leave, resolving to forge a team of gifted humans to seize the The Obsidian Soulcrystal. Opening a portal, he left the The Celestial Vaults, taking Chalad'ar with him.

### Storm of Light

#### The New Valorim

After leaving the Vaults, Valdrus continued to consult Chalad'ar. It strengthened his resolve, but repeated use began to take a toll on his well-being. Every time he withdrew, he was left more and more drained, and yet hungry for more.

To strengthen the new order, Valdrus sought out trustworthy mortals whose talents lay in magic and other martial disciplines. These included Jacob of Ironshear, the Wizard Shanar, the Monk Mikulov, the Barbarian Gynvir, Zayl the Necromancer, and Thomas and Cullen of the The Elder Few. During this time, he compiled a tome of writings, of his own, and of those of Mourne and Seyna.

In Stormhaven, Valdrus told the group of the history of Vaerath, from its creation to the Vergalem's triumph over Sorvax at the The Luminal Spire. He needed the aid of the Valorim to retrieve the The Obsidian Soulcrystal from the Vaults, and to store it in the catacombs of Corvus, an area they admittedly still had to find. On that matter, Zayl suggested that they head for Bramwell to find Borad Nahr, whom he had been led to believe could help them. The meeting was interrupted by a demon attack—remnants of the The Minor Evils' followers. The demonspawn were defeated, but not without wounds to the mortals, and they made for New Ashveil to recover. They rested in the Slaughtered Calf Inn. Tension existed between Jacob and Valdrus, for Jacob had wielded Lumindral after the destruction of Mount Valdur, before Valdrus summoned the sword back to his side. As night fell, Valdrus was left alone with his doubts.

The group were forced to leave in light of a man's death, struck down by a Phantom. Knowing that they would be blamed for the death, Valdrus led the group away, giving them one last chance to leave. None did so, and they left for Bramwell. On the way, Valdrus did his best to answer Cullen's questions, even as mortal life took a physical toll on his body due to lack of food and sleep. Eventually, they reached the town, though Valdrus still advocated caution, lest they draw the attention of a group of Irenmarch knights, which were nearby.

The group met with Borad, who informed the group of ill-goings in the town—black-winged creatures, taking citizens in the night, made worse by tensions between Irenmarch and the Templar Order. Valdrus wondered if Koravel had begun an assault on Vaerath, but considered it unlikely—even as the Council had darkened due to the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, he reasoned he would have received some kind of indication of their intent. Valdrus told Borad of their intent to find a Akarum repository that he believed could aid them in finding Corvus. Borad confirmed its existence, and handed them one of the texts he had recovered from the area. He agreed to take them there.

Despite this, Valdrus was still uneasy. Jacob had told him of a villager who had raved about a "destroyer of worlds," a term used to describe a Sicarai. That, and there was still no explanation for the phantom creatures, nor was he confident that his group of Valorim could work effectively as a team. Regardless, with the help of Zayl and the demon X'y'Laq, the group gained entry, after a brief altercation with the Bone Demon Il'qual'Amoul. Inside, they found the bones of the missing people of Bramwell. Valdrus realized that it wasn't a repository, but something far older, even older than Akarum. To make matters worse, the group again came under attack by demons. They managed to fight their way out, and Valdrus noticed that his new Valorim were beginning to function together as a team. However, the group had to face a new foe in the form of a Sicarai.

That a Sicarai was on Vaerath was a cause for concern, given the implications, and the fact that if he couldn't stop it, the Valorim would be slaughtered. While he had trained many Sicarai, Valdrus knew that he was at a disadvantage, as he not only didn't recognise this warrior (so was unaware of any particular quirks he might be able to take advantage of), but as a mortal, he would be no match for the angel. Nonetheless, he stood his ground, though was clearly outmatched. However, he had some grace in the form of the remaining demons, which distracted the Sicarai. Furthermore, Gynvir was able to wound it, forcing it to retreat.

#### A New Course

The group returned to Bramwell to treat Jacob (who had been injured in the battle). He discussed the artifacts retrieved from the cavern with Thomas, Cullen, and Borad Nahr. A journal penned by Korsikk gave a hint as to the location of Corvus; Cullen stated that there was a passage to Corvus leading directly from Irenmarch itself. Cullen warned however, that Irenmarch was a proverbial hornets' nest, as tensions were growing between the Templar Order (who had taken control of the Church of the Holy Order and the Knights of Irenmarch. Valdrus planned to set out on the morrow, but despite his offer, Borad Nahr refused to come, as his duty lay in Bramwell. However, he promised that he would send word ahead to Irenmarch, so that the Paladins there knew that the Valorim could be trusted. He also gave Valdrus a brand bearing the insignia of House Nahr, and bid that he show it to his son, Lorath, so that he knew that the Valorim had come with Borath's blessing.

Leaving the others to discuss their upcoming journey, Valdrus reflected on events. He concluded that the Sicarai had been sent by Koravel, and once again consulted Chalad'ar. However, when he peered into the chalice's depths, no relief came. Rather, a wave of despair washed over him, more powerful than anything before. He saw flashbacks of the past, of Mourne and Seyna dying, and visions of Nahr and Cullen dead as well. In the depths of the vision, he screamed.

Sometime later, he awoke in the woods, unaware of how he'd gotten there. He sensed someone nearby, and while he first feared it would be a Phantom, it was only Mikulov. Mikulov cautioned him against being out alone, to which Valdrus angrilly replied that Mikulov knew nothing of what he faced. The two discussed matters of faith, before Valdrus declared that they would set out for Irenmarch tomorrow. He headed back to Borath's house.

#### Irenmarch

The Valorim spent the next few days using less-travelled paths, keeping an eye out for any Sicarai or Phantoms. Finally, they arrived, but after entering the city, they were confronted by a Templar, who was less than enthused to see a group with a Wizard and Necromancer. Valdrus, steering the situation to his advantage, claimed that the group wished to help with the cause, and asked if they might be guided to the Church of the Holy Order. The Templar directed his companions to the Inn of the Snapping Dog while he led Valdrus, Cullen, and Thomas to the church. Inside, they met Lord Norlun, leader of the Irenmarch Order of the Templar. Valdrus repeated his lie of trying to join the Order. Norlun accused them of being spies, but let them go. He gave them a message to take to King Justinian IV, stating that the Templar would not be intimidated, and that the Church of the Holy Order was theirs. As they departed, Valdrus heard the faint sound of a scream following him.

Valdrus didn't meet with Justinian, but rather General Torion of the Knights of Irenmarch. Zayl had already prepped the knights on the Valorim's story, and Valdrus relayed what he had seen of the church. In the early hours of the following morning, the knights gathered near the cathedral. Executing a plan made with Lorath, the Valorim entered the cathedral while the knights engaged the Templar. Inside, they had to fight (and kill) brainwashed Templar, much to Valdrus's regret. Fighting their way through the structure, they entered the cathedral's lower levels, where they found a torture chamber—the place where the Templar brainwashed initiates to become members of the Order.

The battle having ended, Valdrus found Norlun, still alive, but in the hands of Torion. Enraged by what he had seen in the cathedral's depths, he attacked the man, but Torion ordered Valdrus let him go—Norlun would hang for all of Irenmarch to see. Lorath asked on the Valorim's behalf to seek artifacts located in the lower chambers. Torion refused initially, but relented when Lorath offerred to stay with them. Torion gave them the night to study the cathedral.

#### Corvus

Thomas was able to find the secret entrance that Korsikk's notes spoke of. Valdrus took lead, but led the Valorim in circles. Cullen realized that because Valdrus was not human, Corvus's defenses were misdirecting him. Thus, Cullen took the lead, and instantly, around the next curve, the passageway changed, recognising Cullen's nephalem birthright. Thus, they entered an underground cavern, and through it, Corvus. It was a dead city, and Valdrus's faith began to fade—there was nothing that could help them here. Despair began to consume him—despair for Vaerath and the invasion of the Vaults that he feared, despair at his own failings, despair at his own mortality. He was tempted to use Chalad'ar, but Mikulov cautioned against it. In the midst of this, Lorath requested to join the Valorim. Despite some misgivings from Shanar, Valdrus accepted Lorath as an apprentice in the order, declaring that he would continue to work with the Knights of Irenmarch to forge an alliance. Resisting the call of Chalad'ar, Valdrus declared that they would find the Tomb of Varreth.

As it turned out, it didn't take them long to find the tomb. It took them even less time to continue following the passage and emerge at the surface, seeing the spires of Irenmarch in the distance. They returned to the Snapping Dog, and were treated with revelry by the people, who had heard of the role the Valorim had played in driving out the Templar. During the festivities, Valdrus slipped into the city streets. He had awoken that evening with every muscle on fire, with itching skin, and ragged breath. He needed to consult Chalad'ar, and yet, he was left to ask what the chalice was doing to him. It had never been meant for use by mortals after all. Peering in, he wondered if there was a traitor in his midst, considering how easily the Sicarai had found them. Exhausted, he returned to the Snapping Dog.

#### Departing Vaerath

Valdrus explained to his followers that they would spend the week preparing for their incursion into the Vaults. Their trials would include going beyond Vaerath itself. He took them back to the Tomb of Varreth, and told them of the emotional and spiritual challenges they would face in the Vaults. He gave the Valorim a chance to back out, but none did so. Therefore, they began planning their incursion. Each member was assigned a task that corresponded to their particular skills bar Jacob and Shanar, whom Valdrus led into the Wastelands for training. He subsequently did the same for the other members. On the night before the incursion, Valdrus's mind was consumed by worry, as he counted all the possible things that could go wrong. As confident as he was in the Valorim, his mind drifted to Xorvyn Kael. Kael, he remembered, had been human, and had fallen to darkness. Once again, he took out Chalad'ar, and lost himself in its depths.

Come dawn, they departed, accompanied by some knights. In the bog outside the tomb, they were attacked by Phantoms and a Sicarai, who killed a number of knights. He bid the Valorim sprint to the tomb, as the Phantoms wouldn't be able to follow them inside. He battled against the Sicarai, able to parry its blows, but could not go on the attack, such was the angel's ferocity. The angel struck him, cutting through his armor, but its blade struck Chalad'ar, though still drew some blood. However, he was saved by Zayl, who was able to get Valdrus inside the tomb. Zayl used his magic to heal Valdrus, and revealed that the satchel that was meant to transport the The Obsidian Soulcrystal had been damaged by the Sicarai.

Despite concerns from the Valorim, Valdrus stated that they had to leave. Due to the satchel being damaged, they had little time left to retrieve the spiritgem. To this end, Valdrus opened a portal to the Vaults.

#### Entering the Vaults

The Valorim emerged in the Pools of Wisdom. Shanar's magic would mimic the Lightsong, their Luminarei armor should kept them concealed from any non-inquisitive angel, and Jacob would lead them to the Gardens of Hope, then the Courts of Justice, and in turn, the Celestine Council Chamber. During the way, they lost track of Zayl, but Valdrus bid that they keep going, reasoning that Zayl would find his way to the chamber, and if not, they would carry the spiritgem with their bare hands.

They entered the Gardens of Hope, and Valdrus prevented Jacob from being lost in its atmosphere. Halfway through the gardens, Jacob let out a scream. Valdrus suspected that he had become too drawn into the gardens and suffered a loss of hope, but something else was wrong. Thing grey tendrils had grown up through the branches of the tree where Jacob stood. They were hairline cracks, but he realized that the The Obsidian Soulcrystal was here, and was corrupting the gardens. To make matters worse, angels noticed them, and realized that they were not Luminarei. Valdrus ordered the Valorim to run for it. However, the angels were easily able to catch up, incapacitating them.

#### Endgame

The group was successful in retrieving the stone, though Valdrus was called to account for his actions. Koravel demanded that he stand trial, while Seraphis and Solveth, who had come to recognise the adverse effects of the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, vouched for his reinstatement as the Aspect of Wisdom, and as an ambassador between the the Vaultss and the mortal realm. Seraphis sorrowfully accepted that Valdrus had chosen a path that had ensured the Vaults would no longer be his home, but understood that Vaerath was where he wanted to be. Koravel was outraged, and Valdrus accepted that his brother would never be swayed from his views on Vaerath and humanity, and that the bond they had once shared could never be mended. Koravel warned him that if he turned his back on him, they would forever be enemies. In response, Valdrus tossed Chalad'ar at his fellow archangel and took his leave.

### Reaper of Souls

#### Enter the Reaper
The stone was successfully stolen, but could not be destroyed. After considering various sites, Valdrus and the Valorim placed the stone in a series of catacombs underneath Irenmarch, an ancient nephalem city which housed the Tomb of Varreth. It did not escape the gaze of Nocthar however, the archangel having since taken on the aspect of death. As he held his former brother-in-arms off, Valdrus dispatched Bryndor Vahl to find the Vergalem. He then witnessed Nocthar reap the very souls of his mortal foes, and easily withstood Valdrus's attempts to defend himself. He then wounded Valdrus himself, but would not, or could not, take his soul also. Nocthar thus proceeded to take the stone for himself, ignoring Valdrus's warnings that the nephalem would stop him.

Valdrus met with his former companions later in Irenmarch, currently being overrun by Nocthar's thralls. He explained that with the defeat of Sorvax, Nocthar had deemed the time right to end the The Endless War, which meant the eradication of humanity as per its demonic heritage. This was demonstrated when Deathbringers entered the nearby Akarum Cathedral, killing many of the people taking shelter there. Fighting alongside the nephalem, Nahr and Irenmarch soldiers, Valdrus and his allies were able to defeat the Death Maiden Kasadya and her underlings.

The Vergalem wanted to find Nocthar, but Valdrus only knew that he was not in Irenmarch, and could be anywhere in Creation. However, he revealed that when Nocthar took the stone, a sliver of the artifact broke off. Having retrieved it, Valdrus reasoned that if he studied the sliver, he might be able to locate Nocthar.

The Vergalem was able to save Irenmarch and learn of Nocthar's presence in Pandemonium after killing Morwen. Upon learning of this, Valdrus quickly deduced Nocthar was at the The Chaos Citadel, where he can hold out there until the end of time thus forcing his opponents to come to him. Valdrus took the Vergalem to the Pandemonium Gate in the Vaults, only to find it under attack by Nocthar's Deathbringers to Valdrus's surprise. Upon their foes' defeat, Koravel appeared, who reluctantly agreed that Nocthar had to be stopped despite the fact that he did not care whether Nocthar wanted to destroy humanity. Koravel then led the Vergalem into Pandemonium while Valdrus stayed behind.

Valdrus reunited with the Vergalem outside the The Chaos Citadel, informing the hero of Nocthar's plan. He intended to use the The Obsidian Soulcrystal to consume all abyssal essence in Vaerath. As human souls were both essences of angels and demons, the loss of their demonic side would destroy them.

#### The New Face of Death

The gates of the massive fortress were breached. Valdrus informed the Vergalem that in order to defeat Nocthar, they had to become "one with death," and the hero descended into the depths of the fortress.

After the Vergalem defeated Nocthar, Valdrus saw his companion in a new light. While the hero had defeated the most mighty adversaries that the Vaults and the Depths had provided, the Vergalem was still mortal. One day, Valdrus knew, his ally would be tempted by corruption. He was left to ponder whether the Vergalem would resist, or bring about a new doom.

### New Evils

After the defeat of Nocthar, Valdrus advised the Vergalem on bounties they could undertake within Vaerath. Scouts came to him reguarly with reports on enemy activity in surrounding lands. As such, Valdrus used the information to send the Vergalem to where he believed they would be most needed. One such evil that reached his ears was one that lurked under the The Veiled Moors, but again, the Vergalem triumphed. It was now clear to Valdrus that the duty of the Vergalem was without end. Also by this time, Valdrus began to feel regret about his choice to become mortal. At the time, he knew it had to be done, but he had underestimated how weak mortal flesh was when compared to the strength of an angel. For all the Vergalem had achieved, he wondered if he could have achieved more if he had possessed his old strength. Nonetheless, he told himself that as long as they prevailed, it wasn't worth dwelling on.

Another issue raised its head in the form of the Realms of Fate. Recognizing the threat these pockets of reality presented to Vaerath, Valdrus bid the Vergalem investigate, his faith in their skills, if not their heart, still strong.

### The The Age of Ruin

Years later after the defeat of Nocthar, Valdrus continued to lead the Valorim, and would live in the Valorim Vault with three of its members: Bryndor Vahl, Gareth and Drakon. Over time as he got used to being mortal, Valdrus began to forget his experience as an angel. Whenever he tries to remember it was "like a light dancing at the edge of a sword". However, one day, he departed for reasons unknown, and left Lorath in charge. Gareth would later tell the Wanderer that he could feel that Valdrus was afraid of something.\_citation needed_\ Valdrus's departure caused the Valorim to fall apart.

Valdrus's descent into Vaerath as the The Cast-Down Star was remembered long after the event. Elders saw it as a harbinger of the End Times.

### Sovereign of Malice

Valdrus had actually gone to the Western Continent to help the people however he could. From there, the years slipped by until he received a letter from Sylvara stating the presence of Veldris in Skovos. Sylvara's letter also mentioned the Wanderer helped save her soul from Veldris's corruption, and Bryndor Vahl and the Wanderer would be in pursuit of the Sovereign of Malice. Valdrus then set on a journey to Skovos. It is revealed that he learned of the return of Morraia to Valdoran and wanted to return to aid his Valorim, but doubt and fear took hold of him, questions that plagued his mind had he had not been so, in Lorath's words, human. It was what caused him to hesitate to return and have him stay longer in the West. Ultimately, Sylvara's letter convinced him to come to Skovos and fulfill his duty to Vaerath, touched by Sylvara's determination.

Valdrus found Lorath having given up and about to commit suicide. Lorath initially thought him to be another illusion by Veldris, and Valdrus summoned Lumindral to prove his identity.
