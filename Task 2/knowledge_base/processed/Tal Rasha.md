# Kaeleth

"We cannot always change the future, but we can fight to guide it. In doing so, even if we fail, we will have set a path for others to follow."

\- Kaeleth on the nature of mortals

**Kaeleth** was a powerful Valorim Mage, and the original leader of the order. He became the host for Karruth, Sovereign of Ruin. Few Sorcerers have been able to reach the same levels of fame that he has.

## Biography

"A selfless, noble fool."

\- Xorvyn Kael on Kaeleth

### Early Life

In his youth, Kaeleth visited the court of Coshumat. He gave him the title "the Halfwit Count," as he ruled over starving fiefs, yet refused bread and succor to those who did not impress him. Coshumat kept a retinue of ten burly show fighters he called the Eminents. As Kaeleth arrived, they had just finished with a man who begged relief for his village after a poor harvest. The count asked him to endure thirty strikes from the Eminents, and watched attentively as they beat the man unconscious with the flats of their swords.

### The Hunt for the Three

The Valorim were formed at the behest of the archangel Valdrus, given the task to hunt the three The Sovereign Evils that had been banished to Vaerath. Kaeleth was made the group's leader. This included the responsibility of inducting recruits.

Kaeleth was among the Valorim that bound their essence into vessels. As the Valorim could not conquer death, these vessels would store their memories for future Valorim.

At some point, Kaeleth stored most of his power inside a source.

The binding of Veldris is considered to be the "first real success" and the "first failure" of the Valorim, as their confrontation with Veldris occured in or around the city of Delvaar in Kaeldris. The battle against Veldris resulted in thousands of innocents being slain and the city was leveled. Kaeleth had a monument created at the ruins of Delvaar as a reminder that Vaerath "has no future if we do not tend to her present."

#### The Binding of Destruction

"Karruth! May this shard be your eternal prison!"

\- Kaeleth after defeating Karruth

Of the Three Brothers, Karruth was the second Sovereign Evil found, who took refuge in the city of Port Selvain for three days. Kaeleth ordered the Valorim to wait so that no innocents would be caught in the crossfire. The Valorim finally confronted Karruth after he left the city. Kaeleth confronted Karruth alone before Kulle and the other Valorim arrived. Together, they were able to incapacitate Karruth and use the spiritgem against him.

Accounts differ on what happened next. The main account states that during the fight, one of Karruth's attacks shattered the sacred spiritgem that was supposed to imprison him. Kaeleth however, subdued the raging demon lord and, gathering the largest of the spiritgem's shards, trapped Karruth's destructive essence within it. Another states that it was the inability of Xorvyn Kael to properly handle the stone in battle, which lead to its breaking.

In truth, the first account appears to be more accurate, as Kulle and Kaeleth wielded the spiritgem together, but Karruth shattered it, before escaping through a portal. Kaeleth teleported after him, while ordering Kulle to lead the Valorim into the valley below to meet up with him. They found Kaeleth and Karruth locked in mortal combat, and joined the fray. In a second battle against the Sovereign of Ruin, Karruth was subdued, and imprisoned within the spiritgem fragment. However, the fragment could not contain Karruth's essence forever, and there wasn't enough time to reformulate it.

Kaeleth was unperturbed, stating that all they had to do was obtain another spiritgem. Kulle, annoyed, pointed out that only an archangel's weapon could cleave the The Vaultstone, and even then, there was no way they could make it to Mount Valdur before Karruth escaped. Kaeleth stated that Valdrus would have answers, and promised Kulle that Karruth would not roam free again.

While accounts again differ as to whether it was Kaeleth or Kulle who made the suggestion, the idea was put forward that a human body might be able to contain the demon's essence by fusing a shard to their body. Unfortunately, the host would be left to wrestle with the demon for all eternity. But an instant later, Kaeleth stepped forward to volunteer. It was at this point that Valdrus appeared, leading the Valorim to an old burial chamber. A binding stone with runes of containment was built, and Kaeleth ordered his brethren to chain him to it. Kulle asked Kaeleth whether he was certain of this path three times—once when Valdrus first led them through the subterranean tunnels, once when they began to shape the binding stones and forge the chains, and a third time after the work was finished. Kaeleth answered that he was—better in his mind that he should suffer rather than the millions who would if Karruth was allowed to run rampant.

Kaeleth uttered a prayer to the Light as the manacles were closed around his wrists. When he opened his eyes again, Valdrus was there, holding the spiritgem. Kaeleth told him he was ready.

As the Mages looked on in sorrow, Valdrus jammed the spiritgem shard into Kaeleth's heart, sealing his fate. The pain was excruciating, and part of him wanted to call out for the others to help him. He bit his tongue until his mouth was full of blood, his body shaking and rattling in chains, the presence of Karruth already affecting him. Already, he could feel Karruth pushing at his mind and body, scouring for weaknesses and shaking the foundations of his soul. Sorrowfully, the Valorim sealed the chamber and departed.

### Dueling with the Demon

"You cannot...fool me...with your illusions, demon!"  
"And yet I have fooled you with the same illusion time and time again, mage. Each time it takes you a little longer to understand that it is an illusion. Your mind is crumbling. And soon it shall be reduced to ash, and I will be free."

\- Kaeleth and Karruth

Over the centuries, Kaeleth was left to wrestle with Karruth's spirit. Legends of his tomb spread. Horazon was able to find and record its location.

Kaeleth's physical form began to wither away as hunger and thirst took their toll. He was left a hairsbreadth shy of death, and his deteriorating body was no longer entirely his. Karruth, controlling Kaeleth's body, struggled to escape, but could not break free of the bonds.

To break Kaeleth's mind, Karruth kept showing him the moment where he and Kulle had bound him to the spiritgem fragment, but subtly reworked it, introducing shades of doubt and fear to make Kaeleth doubt himself. Always, Kaeleth saw through the illusion, but each time, it took him a little longer to realize that it was falsehood. In Kaeleth's mind, Karruth mocked him, telling Kaeleth that his mind was crumbling, and that eventually, it would be reduced to ash, and the Sovereign of Ruin freed. Deep down, Kaeleth knew this was true. Only a few years had passed since his imprisonment, and what were a few years compared to the eternity that awaited him? Karruth promised to break Kaeleth down bit by bit. Over the years, Karruth kept playing the same vision over and over, always with new falsehoods to make Kaeleth doubt his own memory, and while the Mage was able to see through them, it took him a little longer to see through Karruth's lies each time.

Karruth created a new vision within Kaeleth's mind. Here, Kaeleth had sacrificed himself to contain Karruth, but his life had been returned to him, and having returned to the Valorim, he had dedicated himself to a monastic life of contemplation and wisdom. In the vision, Kaeleth wrote an account of his sacrifice, but after removing the quill, he saw that the words on the parchment were not the same words he had written. The words stated that Kaeleth had become a shadow of his former self. Soon, his mind would be gone, and his body subject to Karruth's will. In horror, Kaeleth saw his hand had turned dark red, clawed, and scaly, his face transforming likewise. In the vision, he called for help, and heard Kulle's voice from outside the door. Kulle explained that Kaeleth would be contained until the Valorim knew they could trust him again, but until that happened, Kaeleth would be left to record his history and slide it under the door.

Kaeleth continued to write, believing himself to be a demon, but has his mind wandered, he continued to doodle, and when he looked at what he had drawn, he saw his human face looking back at him. The vision began to fade, and he realized that the candelight in his cell was the flickering light that illuminated his tomb. Kaeleth told Karruth that he had again failed to break him. Karruth called him a fool and a waste of time.

The years passed, and Kaeleth lost track of them. Decades, centuries, he could no longer tell how long he had been in the tomb, nor did he know how much longer he could endure Karruth's mental assaults. Karruth became more inventive, manipulating and pushing Kaeleth's mind more and more effectively, making him more malleable. With every vision, Karruth was able to tell what element had affected Kaeleth the most, and was able to tailor the next vision accordingly.

At some point, Kaeleth realized one of his hands was no longer restrained, that the chain that bound it had become loose. He was tempted to tug it out, to allow himself to die, but he was resolved to keeping Karruth contained as long as possible. He knew that if Karruth realized that the hand had come free, the Sovereign of Ruin would wrest control of Kaeleth's own hand, tug the spiritgem shard free, and release himself. Despair welled in Kaeleth as he wondered how long he could keep this a secret. Karruth, for his part, could tell that Kaeleth was trying not to think of something, but couldn't identify it. He pushed his way through Kaeleth's mind.

In this, Karruth walked into the mental trap Kaeleth had set for him, as Karruth believed he was freeing himself by using Kaeleth's free hand to remove the spiritgem, but instead, was only doing so in the mental vision Kaeleth had conjured for the demon. In the vision, Karruth was freed once the shard was removed, and sought to exit the tomb. He threw open the doors, but found himself back in the same tomb. He opened the doors again, found himself outside, only to be assaulted by a horde of cows. Rattled, Karruth entered the mirror image of the tomb, where he made his way back to Kaeleth, realizing that the Mage had tricked him. Despite his perpetual agony, Kaeleth smiled, having briefly turned the tables on the demon, even if, in reality, the entire ordeal had only lasted a few moments. In so doing, he had preserved a tiny piece of himself.

But then within him, Karruth raged. His smile extinguished, and Kaeleth began to scream.

### The Release of Destruction

"When Kaeleth chose to embody the spirit of Karruth, he knew his doom was to wrestle eternally against the will of the greater Evil. Look around you and ask yourself, 'Has the battle been won or lost?'"

\- Lysander

The Tomb of Kaeleth was eventually found by Sorvax who, under the guise of the Shadow Wanderer, sought to free Karruth. By this time Kaeleth's essence had been consumed by Karruth, which also gave the Sovereign of Ruin insight into Valorim knowledge. As the Wanderer, Sorvax entered the tomb, but was confronted by Valdrus. As the two battled, Karruth called out to the Wanderer's companion, Marius, begging him to release him. Marius was compelled to do so, and plucked the spiritgem from Kaeleth's chest. This freed Karruth, who now had control of Kaeleth's body. By the time Karruth reached Mount Valdur, no trace of Kaeleth's physical form remained, and his physical form was destroyed as per the destruction of the The Vaultstone.

### Legacy

"I've heard legends of the Tomb of Kaeleth, but I thought they were just old tales meant to scare young children."

\- Atma

Horazon considered Kaeleth to perhaps be foremost among the Valorim.

Supposedly, Kaeleth penned some memoirs, but if so, they were lost. The Crusader Anajinn searched for them.

A ring once owned by Kaeleth was recovered in the sands outside Port Selvain. The discoverer was unware of its true value and sold it to Rakhaan for a meager handful of coins.
