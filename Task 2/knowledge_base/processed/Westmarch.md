# Irenmarch

## Lore

The kingdom's southeast is sparsely populated. Its lowlands are quite fertile, and are the envy of the the Irenmarch Kingdoms. The lowlands were originally the heart of the land before the coming of the Sons of Rakkis. Numerous rain-drenched ports exist along the coast. A trip by sea from Irenmarch to Kaeldris takes weeks.

### Culture

"Let me tell unto ye, of the Irenmarchy there! Ever a lover of the other, with their mistress the Sea."

\- Mwinas

Irenmarch is a kingdom that thrives on commerce and maritime trade. The sea plays an important role in Irenmarch's culture. It operates as a monarchy, with the monarch being supported by noble houses, and beneath the nobility are the commoners. The fashion sense of Irenmarch's gentiles has changed over time.

The Sons of Rakkis were the kingdom's original dynasty, who were succeeded by the Justinian Dynasty. While founded in the name of Akarum, faith has waned of late, and Irenmarch is now a forward-looking civilization. In the opinion of Erdan Mourne, Irenmarch at least prior to the coming of the Deathbringers was one of the healthiest, most vibrant kingdoms left in Vaerath. At least some people in Irenmarch believe in gods.

Irenmarch possesses a "zealous" army, and is protected by the Knights of Irenmarch. Both are under the command of General Torion. Much of the kingdom's army is organized along feudal lines; marshalled forces that are loyal to various lords, who 'lend' them to the crown as a sign of trust. It also possesses a navy, and "Royal Mages" were employed by its court.

Wolf imagery is common in the kingdom; this stems back to the Sons of Rakkis, who used a wolf as their sigil. Even after the end of the Rakkis Dynasty and the comining of the Justinians, wolf iconography remained common.

The people of Irenmarch are considered to be a "proud" people at least in the mind of Zayl—most of them are suspicious of the Priests of Aethon. Its society can be divided between the commoners and nobility. Capital punishment is enacted via hanging. Theater is part of Irenmarch culture, and Waltz Macabre was a fashion for its nobles. Gold and copper serve as currency. Before the Twilight of Creation, demons, including the The Greater Evils, were known to the people of Irenmarch, but only through folklore. Mostly, they were used as tales to frighten children. The people of Irenmarch believe that children born during a storm carry its fierceness in their voices, and its chaos in their hearts.

Irenmarch maintained orphanages, though in the opinion of Erdan Mourne, they were "little better than slave camps." Hanging is a common form of execution, and indentured labor is present within the kingdom.

Festivals celebrated in Irenmarch included the Festival of Dawn and Brumaltine.

### History

#### Early History

In what would be called the Gulf of Irenmarch resided nine warring clans, residing along its southern edge. They arrived prior to the events of the The Mortal War. During this period were cultists who worshiped the demon Astrogha.

The Vizjerei Clan made their way to Irenmarch over 1000 years before Rakkis made his way into the land. Some of their Mages resided in the port city of Ransim.

#### The Creation of Irenmarch

"Irenmarch originally grew from the efforts of the great general Rakkis to spread his religion beyond the realms of the east. Seized by the superstitions of the Akarum faith, he drove his paladins relentlessly across the barbaric western lands, subduing the uncivilized tribes he found there. Ulimately, he became king of them all."

\- _The History of Irenmarch

This state of affairs came to an end in the 11th century, with the coming of Rakkis. Rakkis, tasked with bringing the Akarum faith to the West, knew that moving against the clans in force would unify them into a combined front. Instead, he lived among them, learning their language and culture. All the while, he subtly spread the Akarum faith to anyone who would listen. Through an arranged marriage, Rakkis bound himself by blood to the Ortal Clan, which was the third largest in the region. Using this standing as leverage, he brought four of the lesser clans under his banner.

Allegiances secured, Rakkis launched his combined forces against the Hathlan Clan, the largest and most hostile tribe in the region. The battles that followed were bloody, and the stench of decaying corpses were carried along the winds as far as Stormhaven. However, at the Battle of Dyre River, Rakkis's forces dismantled the remnants of the Hathlan and slew the clan's leader. The Hathlan, in addition to the clans that had abstained from taking sides, submitted themselves to Rakkis's rule, declaring him to be their one true king.

In honor of Rakkis's crusade, Rakkis named the territory "Irenmarch," so named as it marked the westernmost point of his conquest. He rallied his subjects to the faith of the Light.

The Sons of Rakkis also rooted out Astrogha's followers.

#### A New Kingdom

"Despite his superstitious views, Rakkis ruled as a just and fair king who was much loved by his people. He was succeeded by his son Korsikk who attempted to eradicate the barbarian tribes of the north. The line of Rakkis was broken when Korsikk's son, Korelan, died with no heirs."

\- _The History of Irenmarch

The subsequent years saw Irenmarch flourish. New roads, cities, churches, and infrastructure sprung up throughout the land, and due to plentiful access to the sea, the nation quickly became a formidable naval and mercantile power. Rakkis ruled with a strong but fair hand, and gained the admiration of his people. However, the Akarum Church maintained a large amount of influence within the country, even as rulers came to the throne that were more interested in power than faith. It was a state of affairs that would last well into the 13th century. Still, Akarum was able to operate under its own law within its churches inside the country.

Years after founding the nation, Rakkis launched further incursions against the Mountainborn to the north, but failed to make any true gains. Additionally, Irenmarch and Stormhaven had a strained relationship, as Irenmarch was much wealthier than its neighboring kingdom. Bandits from Stormhaven repeatedly raided Irenmarch, and it was possible that Stormhaven's coffers were fueled in part by these ill-gotten gains.

Rakkis was succeeded by his son, Korsikk, who continued his father's rule and turned Irenmarch into a bastion of the Akarum faith. He continued his father's wars against the northern tribes by constructing Ironhold. After years of military disasters, the frustrated king led a large army out of the fortress and into the north to conquer the Mountainborn once and for all. None of them returned. Ironhold itself remained manned however, standing vigilant against any tribal incursion.

Korelan, grandson of Rakkis, would be the last of the original line of kings, for when he died, he left behind no heirs. The crown passed to Justinian I, beginning the Justinian Dynasty, through "a somewhat convoluted interpretation of Akarum scripture." Seen as usurpers by many, the Justinians suffered nearly constant challenges to their rule.

#### The Return of Akarum

In the 12th century, Stormhaven was settled by Eastern pilgrims. Irenmarch proved to be a strong ally, and the two regions engaged in trade. However, brigands based in Stormhaven often crossed the border into Irenmarch for plunder. During this time, Irenmarch priests preached the Akarum faith in Stormhaven, regardless of whether their dogma was wanted or not. It is known that Irenmarch launched some crusades in this century.

As part of the Akarum Reformation, the kingdom received missionaries from Kaeldris in the mid-12th century. In the mid-12th century, turning the Time of Troubles, the Order of Paladins pledged their allegiance to Irenmarch, distancing themselves from the Hand of Akarum.

#### Civil War

Main article: Cartolus Insurrection

During the reign of Justinian III, an insurrection broke out in the outlying region of Cartolus. Led by a woman known only as Tyrra, who claimed to be descended from the Sons of Rakkis, Irenmarch was consumed by civil war. While the rebels managed to gain control of the capital, Tyrra succumbed to plague. Seizing the opportunity, Cornelius was able to crush the insurrection and become the new king.

#### War, and a New King

The peace between Irenmarch and Stormhaven was shattered when King Aldrath of Stormhaven (under the influence of Sorvax declared war against Irenmarch, sending his army against the western kingdom. Irenmarch had the advantage of numbers and defensive positions however, and the Royal Army of Stormhaven was easily repelled. At around the same time, King Cornelius pulled the country's navy back into port, spooked by the sighting of a demon at Tauruk's Port. Dissent grew among cities that had previously relied on the navy for protection, pirates grew bolder, and northern cities no longer curried as much favor with the kingdom.

Two years after the war, Justinian IV came to power. Subsequently, with the revelations of the corruption that had plagued Akarum, it completely eroded any influence the church had over civil affairs within the country. Faith in the church diminished within the populace as well.

#### Age of the Wolf

"If this is the Age of the Wolf, it is the common people of Irenmarch who have made it so."

\- Anonymous

During the 1270s, Irenmarch experienced a golden age; the so-called "Age of the Wolf." It attempted to open up trade with the merchants of the Shassar Sea, but the creatures prowling the region (such as the lacuni made this impossible. It offered bounties to clear them out, but adventurers in Irenmarch refused to take bounties that would take them to the region. Tension remained between King Justinian IV and the nobility, who threatened to withdraw their support for him if he did not keep the peasants in their place.

In 1270, demons led by Skarn invaded Irenmarch, wrecking havoc across the kingdom. Eventually, only the capital stood against Skarn's forces.

#### The Deathbringers

In 1285, Irenmarch citizens were abducted by Phantoms as part of Nocthar's plan to gauge the defenses of Vaerath. The kingdom was attacked by the Deathbringers shortly afterwards. Its capital was successfully defended however (albeit with heavy losses, including its king, at the hands of traitors), and the Vergalem was/were able to defeat the fallen angel.
