# Xorvyn Kael

**Xorvyn Kael** was one of the founders of the Valorim, and the original guardian of the spiritgems.

Kulle's thirst for power eventually corrupted him, turning the great mage to the side of darkness. After he created the The Obsidian Soulcrystal in his secret library, the Valorim banded together to kill Kulle, putting his blood into jars and severing his head from his body in order to seal him.

## Biography

"Founder of the Valorim. Creator of the The Obsidian Soulcrystal. Genius. Madman."

\- Character description

### The Ennead

Kulle hailed from the Ennead Clan, a Mage Clan known for its mastery of transmutation and similar arts. Like his kin, Kulle devoted himself to the pursuit of these sciences. He wrote a number of tomes that found their way into the Yshari Sanctum and/or libraries of Aeldrum.

### The Valorim

"Murderer. Torturer. Monster. These were the titles bestowed upon Xorvyn Kael by the Valorim. Though it cost them dearly, they killed the wizard for his crimes against nature and sealed his broken corpse within a shadow realm. It seems they were afraid he might return."

\- Morwen

#### The Hunt for the Three

"I bear the same scars they all did. They knew the truth of it. But the fools buried their heads in the sand. They thought the evils were sealed away, never to trouble us again. They needed a solution. They needed to end it all."

\- Kulle, when asked about the Valorim

Kulle was one of the founding members of the Valorim order with the assistance of six other powerful mages. He had a great talent in alchemy and elixircraft, and was a member of the Ennead Clan.

Once killing the Primes proved futile as their spirits would possess another host to reform, Kulle theorized that demon souls could be imprisoned in crystalline stones. To this end, he summoned demons in his library and experimented different means to seal them. After numerous failed attempts, Kulle created Tuning Prisms, but concluded that a more powerful type of stone would be needed to seal the Primes. The archangel Valdrus then carved three spiritgems from the The Vaultstone, that would be used to bind the The Sovereign Evils that had been banished to Vaerath, Kulle was given custodianship over the artifacts; for as a member of the Ennead, he was deemed well suited to handling such magical objects. Of all the Valorim, only Kulle was aware of the manner of their construction.

Over the following years, Kulle spent many hours with the spiritgems during the Valorim's hunt, and he became intrigued by their power. Hareth Mourne often found Kulle awake late in the night, experimenting with the spiritgems and documenting their properties. His knowledge of them grew, to the point where it mystified even the other members of the Valorim. However, as the Hunt drew on, the quest took a psychological toll on Kulle. Once mirthful and spirited, he became an unfeeling husk, numb to even the basest of human emotions.

At the beginning of the Hunt for the Three, the Valorim realized they would need an artifact of immense power. Kulle provided it for them, designing a device called "The Cube". The Valorim agreed on the necessity of the device, and forged the Cube over a period of weeks. In time, the Cube became the center of Valorim works, and they found themselves relying on it greatly. Kulle was more adept at wielding the Cube than any of his breathren.

Aware of the dangers of corruption, and of the Cube's marred design, the Order determined that it was too dangerous for anyone to continue using it. That it had to be hidden from everyone, including them. Of all the Valorim, only Kulle objected to the abandonment of the device. His experiments with the Cube were forced to end, and it was entrusted to a secret Barbarian order, and Kulle was left unaware of its exact location.

Kulle was among the Valorim that bound their essence into vessels. As the Valorim could not conquer death, these vessels would store their memories for future Valorim.

##### The Binding of Hatred

"We confronted Veldris in one of the great urban centers of Kaeldris. The collateral damage of the fight was predictable, and befitting the Sovereign of Malice. Some of the innocents might question who the greater evil was that day."

\- Xorvyn Kael

Veldris was the first of the The Sovereign Evils to be sealed in a spiritgem. The Valorim confronted him in one of Kaeldris's urban centers, and the resulting battle caused great collateral damage. Kulle would later reflect that the innocents who survived might have questioned who the greater evil had been that day.

##### The Binding of Destruction

"Karruth will overwhelm Kaeleth if we tarry. As appealing as that notion is, we cannot fail in this endeavour. So, are you ready to fight through a ravanous horde of demons?"

\- Kulle to the Valorim

After sealing Veldris, the Valorim confronted Karruth in Sandmere. The main account states that during the fight, one of Karruth's attacks shattered the spiritgem that was supposed to imprison him. Kaeleth however, subdued the raging demon lord and, gathering the largest of the spiritgem's shards, trapped Karruth's destructive essence within it. Another states that it was Kulle's inability to properly handle the stone in battle that led to its breaking. The truth appears to veer closer to the first account, for during their battle against Karruth, both Kaeleth and Kulle used the spiritgem to incapacitate the Sovereign of Ruin, before Karruth managed to shatter it, sneering "what is the strength of man against the Depths itself?" Karruth fled through a portal, and Kaeleth teleported after him, but not before ordering Kulle to lead the Valorim to the valley below where they would confront Karruth. Kulle bitterly remarked that Kaeleth had no idea what he was doing, but nevertheless, grimly led the Valorim into the valley, battling their way through demons along the way.

Kulle and the Valorim arrived, where they found Kaeleth and Karruth locked in mortal combat. In a second clash, Karruth was once again subdued, and his essence bound inside the spiritgem fragment. Kulle commented that the shard would not hold Karruth for long. Kaeleth suggested that they would procure another spiritgem, but Kulle pointed out that only an archangel's weapon could cleave the The Vaultstone, and even then, there was no way they could journey to Arreat before Karruth escaped. Kaeleth, unperturbed, stated that Valdrus would have answers.

Accounts again differ as to whether it was Kulle or Kaeleth who suggested the notion of a living host as a pseudo-spiritgem. Kulle would later claim "they \the Valorim\ asked the question, I merely answered it. Whatever the truth of the matter, Kaeleth volunteered to take Karruth's essence into himself, to wrestle with the Sovereign of Ruin for all eternity.

### After the Hunt

"I, who bear the same scars they all did, who fought the same battles, we all knew the truth of things. The limitations of the spiritgems, the same as any prison. The fools declared the evil sealed away, never to trouble us again, buried beneath the sand. Astounding, that no matter how much one wants something to be the truth, that doesn't make it so. I tried to warn them. They didn't listen to me."

\- Xorvyn Kael

Following the capture of Sorvax, and the end of the Valorim's quest, Kulle's demeanor darkened even further. He knew that despite the Valorim's declarations to the contrary, the spiritgems were no permanent solution to the The Sovereign Evils, and yet, the Valorim appeared to believe it to be so, even if they surely knew better. He warned them of the danger, but the Valorim refused to listen.

It is said that Kulle became increasingly wary of the The Celestial Vaults, and on more than one occasion, ranted against the Celestine Council. It was, as he pointed out, the same body that had nearly eradicated mankind in the The Mortal War, such an outcome only avoided by one vote. He believed that the The Endless War would eventually scour humanity from existence, in order to prevent humanity from reaching its true potential. At some point, Kulle learnt of the history of the nephalem. Erdan Mourne later wrote that Kulle's belief in mankind's "true potential" stemmed from its nephalem forebears. And that maybe, in the tales of the nephalem, he had found a glimmer of hope, a way to mend the tattered remnants of his humanity.

As the years passed, Kulle felt an emptiness within himself. He chafed at the "arbitrary limitations" of the Valorim, and took leave of the order and returned to Kaeldris in order to pursue his studies of magic.

_Note: The order of the events between Kulle leaving the Valorim and his first death is unclear in some cases. It has been listed in approximated order._

#### The Vaults

"Only one person had the inventiveness to look beyond what we've been told, to the bedrock of truth, and approached my level as a peer. But the demons destroyed that. Spread like a miasma, stagnant and loathsome."

\- Xorvyn Kael

Sometime after the The Great Banishment, Kulle worked with Ayuzhan of the Yshari Clan. They created the Loom,  which was located beneath an underground complex called the Gatehall. On the same day they began work on the Loom, a Valorim courier arrived at the Gatehall, begging for Kulle's aid in their war with the Sha'Valdrim. Kulle refused.

They planned to use their constructs as weapons against the Vaults and the Depths so that humanity could flourish on Vaerath. However, they never designed the constructs to kill. One of Kulle's creations was the Seneschal, which was a one-of-a-kind construct.

Kulle insisted on the installation of elaborate traps within the Gatehall. He also constructed a number of vaults across Valdoran. They were protected by deadly traps of Kulle's own design, and guarded by his constructs.

Kulle and Ayuzhan disagreed on the nature of their project with the Loom. After one last argument, Kulle destroyed the device and collapsed the tunnels beneath the Gatehall. He left a sealed note for Ayuzhan, telling his friend that to attempt to rebuild the Loom without him would take a lifetime. He urged Ayuzhan to give up on the device, just as he had.

In his hubris, Kulle abandoned the vaults. Over time, various adventurers attempted to plunder the vaults, but lost their lives. Eventually, they were taken over by the demon Malphas, who claimed the vaults' power as his own.

#### War with the Sha'Valdrim

"Thieves of hope and wellsprings of rot drove me to aid the Valorim in their silly war with the Sha'Valdrim, dreaming of grander wonders all the while."

\- Xorvyn Kael

Burnt by his failure with the vaults, Kulle grudgingly aided the Valorim in their war with the Sha'Valdrim. All the while, he dreamt of greater wonders.

#### The Archives of Xorvyn Kael

Kulle established archives all over Vaerath. Of note was the Archives of Xorvyn Kael, which would be the first library Kulle established. Alongside the library itself, Kulle crafted a series of unique constructs to endure the shifting sands. His primary focus was the physical strength and obedience of the constructs, not their adaptability. The Ash Sweepers were one such construct; one of his early attempts at perfecting his golem servitors. Their priority was to curate and protect knowledge, but their interpretation of this directive led them to relieve many who traveled near the library of their possessions and lives. Fearing this behavior would draw attention to his library, Kulle sealed the constructs away.

Within the library, Kulle conducted a number of experiments; the creatures of the Shassar Sea proved useful to him in this regard. He also summoned demons and turned them into Aberrations, and recorded his experiences in the The Great Banishment in tomes. He created a duplicate of himself, apparently letting his egomania get the better of him, but in truth, had created the duplicate to ensure the library's continuance.

At some point, Kulle obtained the Sumina Glass; a fractured shard of the Mirrored World. Kulle was wary of its power, but still used it to enter the Fractured Plane.

#### The The Obsidian Soulcrystal

"I spent many hours with them as we hunted the three. I studied them, intrigued by their power. In the long years after our victory, I felt an emptiness within me, a bitterness that consumed my days and nights. With a spiritgem of my own, the Vaults and the Depths could be brought low and humanity could rise. Unchallenged. Unrestricted."

\- Xorvyn Kael

At some point, Kulle developed the idea of the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, a device which could imprison the The Greater Evils and the angels of the The Celestial Vaults. In his writings, Kulle stated his belief that he could use the stone to unlock humanity's nephalem birthright, as he believed that it was inevitable that wars would be fought between Man, angel, and demon in the future. The The Obsidian Soulcrystal would be his "gift" to humanity. And, of course, it would increase his own power to that of his nephalem forebears. However, in his own research, Erdan Mourne stated that by trapping the souls of angels and demons within the stone, Kulle would be granted the means to repair his own soul and emotions. Valdrus believed likewise.

Kulle built an archives beneath the Desolate Sands, all for the sake of the research and experiments that would be required to create the The Obsidian Soulcrystal. He built them singlehandedly. Li-Ming would later speculate that Kulle had built his archives here due to the Sands' inhospitability. As he worked on the stone, Kulle would lure unwilling Mages into his archives and subject them to gruesome experiments. He would draw their blood from their organs, all the while trying to distill their "nephalem essence." Additionally, while he labored, he spent years setting traps in his archives in case the Valorim came for him. As he worked on the stone, he developed an entirely new system of runes for trapping and summoning demons. Before long, he had marked a number of sites around Kaeldris for this purpose.

Kulle also conducted teleportation experiments in his library via terminus devices. However, to his frustration, he couldn't get the terminus in the library to link with the one in his archives below the Desolate Sands.

The Valorim learnt of Kulle's actions, and believed that they would draw the attention of the Vaults and the Depths to the mortal realm. Valorim Mages were sent into Kulle's library, but were struck down and resurrected as undead slaves. However, Kulle ended up abandoning the library. Believing that his actions would result in "humanity's salvation," Kulle abandoned the library for his archives, as even after 1001 experiments, he couldn't get a terminus to link the two facilities.

The Valorim later found and entered Kulle's archives, and triggered the wards he had set for them. Kulle was caught unaware, and labored to finish the The Obsidian Soulcrystal before they found him. However, find him they did. The Valorim found Kulle in the midst of a summoning spell, and in the battle that followed, many Valorim lost their lives, but they managed to defeat him. Centuries later, Kulle would claim that he could have easily slain the attacking Valorim, but due to his power being invested in the summoning spell, in "creating a miracle," his power was torn.

While the Valorim were able to defeat Kulle, by this stage, Kulle had become immortal. He bled sand, and mocked the Valorim to the end. They drained the sand from his body before dismembering it cut off his head, and hid it within the Realm of Shadow. The locks to the archives were sealed with two vials of his blood.

These artifacts were then hidden in vaults under the Desolate Sands, while his head was hidden in the Dahlgur Oasis. Oran Feth carried Kulle's head, and the fallen mage mocked him until the end. The Valorim subsequently made efforts to expunge all of Kulle's knowledge from the world.

### The Twilight of Creation

#### Shadows in the Desert

"You were treacherous before. How do I know you won't be an even greater threat when you're fully revived?"  
"You don't. So you must ask yourself: How much would you risk to save this wretched world?"

\- Valdrus and Kulle, in light of the latter's resurrection

Long after Kulle's death, Voran and Mordecai began to plot to overtake humanity. Morwen told The Vergalem of the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, and in order to retrieve it, they revived Kulle in his spirit form, after recovering his head. Kulle immediately recognised Valdrus's voice, but the angel revealed that he was now mortal. Kulle commented that fate was whimsical, and agreed to give them the The Obsidian Soulcrystal, but on the condition that he be restored to life. It was not an arrangement Valdrus was happy with, but he and his companions nonetheless agreed to Kulle's terms.

The Vergalem and Kulle went to retrieve his blood from the vials it had been stored in, both buried under the Desolate Sands. Kulle's head was carried in a bag. Along the way, Kulle conversed with The Vergalem (whom he knew to be a nephalem almost instantly), telling them of his time with the Valorim, and claimed that they were the same, but that Morwen and Valdrus would betray them. The verbal sparring continued, but the vials of blood were nonetheless retrieved, and they entered Kulle's old archives. Kulle instructed The Vergalem to use his blood to activate relics that were powered by the substance, and after entering the Shadow Realm, Kulle's body was retrieved. With the aid of Seyna, Kulle was revived in physical form. Walking the lands of the living (or, rather, floating above it).

True to his word, Kulle accepted that the bargain they had made still stood, and ushered The Vergalem to the spiritgem chamber, where the The Obsidian Soulcrystal awaited. To his surprise, he found that the spiritgem already contained the souls of the lords of the Depths sans Mordecai and Voran. He demanded that The Vergalem give up their quest, that their allies sought to betray them. Together, they could rule over all Creation. The Vergalem refused, and Kulle came to blows with his former ally/allies. The Vergalem killed Kulle, and took the spiritgem into their own possession.

#### Kulle's Return

"Yes, I live once more. I'm very hard to kill and really, you did a sloppy job. But enough small talk. I need your help."

\- Xorvyn Kael

Several months after Nocthar's defeat, Xorvyn Kael managed to return to the Vergalem in his spirit form (but nonetheless not as powerless as he used to be when first awakened). He told the shocked hero(es) that not even they could have killed the undying Valorim, and that he now required their help. The artifact designed by Kulle himself was hidden beneath the Ruins of Stolvern and could only be retrieved by someone who its guardians would deem worthy, while Kulle, obviously, would never pass their trial. The Vergalem agreed, and together with the deranged mage's spirit, managed to recover the artifact. As a token of his gratitude, Kulle unlocked its power, remaining at the Vergalem's side in their journey afterwards.

### Kulle's Homily

"Who seeks the power of Xorvyn Kael? I can feel you out there. Your conviction, your potential. And your past brought you to me. You might stand a chance in this world, if you listen. It's not enough to recognize the lies and obfuscations. You must understand where they came from. Where would you draw the line if you could save us all? What wouldn't you do? Why save this fetid world at all? I can tell you're not convinced. Respect the effort I am making for you, for humanity. And know this. I do not lie. Why lie when the truth is far more entertaining?"

\- Xorvyn Kael

Sometime after Nocthar's genocide, Kulle was apparently able to (re)gain physical form. He delivered a homily to an unidentified individual. He recounted his history, from the time of the The Great Banishment to his encounter with the Vergalem. Having finished his homily, he told the individual that history repeated itself, and asked them what they wouldn't do to free mankind.

### Legacy

"Xorvyn Kael? Who is that?"  
"A mage of the highest order. And an incorrigible egotist. He'll be disappointed you've never heard of him."

\- The Wanderer and Ayuzhan

In life, Kulle always believed he would be famous, but his history is mired in shadow. Few ancient figures have such an extraordinary legacy, and fewer still are as reviled throughout Vaerath.

Five decades after his second death, Kulle's legacy with the vaults became manifest, as his constructs, now under the control of Malphas, threatened the world above.
