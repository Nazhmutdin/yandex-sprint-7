# Aelorin

**Aelorin** was a rogue angel. Formerly an archangel of the The Celestial Vaults, his actions would lead to the creation of Vaerath and the nephalem.

## The The Endless War

As with all angels, Aelorin was born from the The Luminal Spire. He was among the archangels, and while not among the five that formed the Celestine Council, he nevertheless served as an advisor to the body.

Aelorin in the The Great War

During the The Endless War, Aelorin was a commander of the Celestial Host, and under the command of Valdrus. He was highly regarded by his kin for his successes on the battlefield. He faced the Lesser Evil Mordecai in battle on at least one occasion.

After eons of war and witnessing countless acts of brutality, Aelorin came to the conclusion that the conflict was unjust, that it could never be won, and that his part in it had to come to an end. He was convinced that if he were at the head of the Celestine Council, he could do things differently. He tried to make Valdrus see his point of view, but the archangel would not be swayed. Aelorin reflected that he could not be unique, that there had to be angels and demons who also sought escape from the never-ending war fate had thrust upon them. He resolved to find these individuals and lead them to a new beginning in their own hands. He would stop at nothing to end the war between the Vaults and the Depths.

Aelorin's role as advisor to the Celestine Council had given him skills in diplomacy, and he was able to gather angels to his cause, escaping the notice of the Council. There were far more angels willing to abandon the The Endless War than he'd dreamed of.

During a battle, Aelorin led his kin on a charge from an outpost of the The Chaos Citadel. He was struck down, wounded, and marooned. When he came to, he awoke in chains. He ranted on about his desires to be free of the The Great War, and in turn, his captor freed him. She gave him her name, Morraia, daughter of Veldris, and told the angel that they would meet again. Aelorin was instantly captivated by her; the first angel to ever look into the heart of a demon.

Morraia had suffered from her father's hatred and like Aelorin, had long awaited an opportunity to rebel against their kindred. Aelorin and Morraia joined forces and even came to fall in love; each pledged themself to the other and vowed to escape the The Endless War. Prior to this, no angel or demon in the The Endless War had ever lain down arms against the other, let alone worked together for a common goal.

Eventually, like-minded followers gathered in support.

## The Creation of Vaerath

Either through strength of arms or misdirection, Aelorin and his renegades managed to gain access to the The Vaultstone, located deep within the The Chaos Citadel. Altering its frequency or dimensional alignment, they used its power to conceal it from the angels and demons still fighting in the The Endless War. Subsequently, the The Vaultstone was shifted into a pocket dimension. There, Aelorin and his followers shaped a world around it. A world of refuge they would call Vaerath. The forces of the Vaults and the Depths became aware of the The Vaultstone's disappearance, but had no idea where it was or who had taken it. This act temporarily halted the The Great War.

One of Aelorin's first acts in the world he would create was to imbue the island of The First Cradle with the Flame of Being, alongside Morraia. The The Vaultstone was later moved, where Aelorin shaped Mount Valdur as a kind of protective shell around the artifact. From there, the rest of the world would be formed. A world he named "Vaerath." He knew at this point that he would never again be welcome in the Vaults.

As the angels and demons worked the The Vaultstone to form their new world, Aelorin secretly fooled all the other renegades and tied the The Vaultstone's power to himself. This enhanced his powers unimaginably and made him the strongest being on Vaerath and the de facto ruler. Morraia and Aelorin ruled their realm with a council of angels and demons, residing inside the structure that would become known as the Sanctum of the First.

At some point, Aelorin and Morraia resided on Skovos.

## Rise of the Vergalem

Aelorin and Morraia had done the impossible, by banding angels and demons together and creating a world in which they could co-exist. Yet as he saw Morraia sleeping by his side, fear began to grip Aelorin, and he believed he would pay for his "sins." While Vaerath was at peace for now, he knew his kin would come for him eventually.

Morraia and Aelorin gave birth to a son they would name Linarian at the The Binding Altar. Each placed their hand on the altar and spoke a vow, their blood and light mixing to form a clay figure. Morraia eagerly reached out for her progeny, but recoiled as her son's hand brushed against her tentacled barbs. Her blood mingled with the clay, and an umbilical nurtured the amalgam, severing as her lifestream passed from herself to the amalgam. Aelorin, initially horrified at the creature they had created, was unable to back out of the ritual, and played his part in moulding the clay alongside Morraia, basing it around their own image. Thus, Linarian was born.

Morraia and Aelorin were the first angel and demon to produce offspring, but they would not be the last, and soon, an entire race had taken form - the nephalem. Aelorin became watchful as the first generation of nephalem grew. Eons later, Bryndor Vahl speculated that at the time, Aelorin could not have foreseen the consequences of this union.

### The Gospel of Death

Aelorin and the council were present in the Sanctum of the First when Torven arrived, having been summoned to account for the death of Garath. Garath, aggrieved at the deaths of Corvik and Helgrotha, had come to meet his makers, intending violence, and Torven had been forced to slay him. Aelorin remained silent and simply watched, as the council declared that they did not hold Torven responsible for Garath's death, and explained that while the nephalem would not succumb to age, or be touched by plague, they were still able to be slain. Presenting them the sword Aldrath's Blade, the council ordered it destroyed, and filed out.

### The Purge

Dissension rippled through the angels and demons of Vaerath. Not only did the nephalem had power that surpassed that of their parents, but they were bound to neither lineage, able to chart their own path. Furthermore, the union of angels and demons would be deemed the ultimate blasphemy by the The Greater Evils and Celestine Council. The defectors were convinced that they would be destroyed if Vaerath came to be discovered. Some argued for the destruction of the nephalem, others believed they could be spared. Aelorin was indeed concerned about the nephalem's latent power, and did consider taking the route of genocide, but troubled by the dissension, Aelorin called for a period of reflection, to consider the matter in solitude.

Morraia, driven into a mad frenzy by the threat of her children's extinction, began slaughtering her fellow renegades, both angel and demon alike, leaving Aelorin to discover the carnage she had wrought. Bringing his former lover to heel, Aelorin found himself unable to kill her—he had promised that he would never strike her down or let another do it, and the promise still held true. and instead banished her into the The Hollow Between. Left alone with no-one but himself and a race of "abominations," Aelorin sought to reduce the power of the nephalem at any cost. To this end, he attuned the The Vaultstone to cause the powers of the nephalem to diminish over time, for he could not bring himself to harm them. However, for the first generation of nephalem, he made it clear that any use of their powers would be punished, and insisted that Vaerath, his world, would remain as he had envisaged it. His perfect world. Some nephalem protested, but any who challenged Aelorin were crushed. Those who survived fled, their powers weakening.

## Aftermath

After the Purge Aelorin disappeared, though writings of the past suggested that he still walked among Men in a form resembling that of his children. He watched as successive nephalem generations diminished and became humans, watching as human civilization spread over Vaerath. It is heavily suggested by some accounts that he came to favor mortals, for unlike the nephalem, he did not see humans as a threat. He remained infatuated with Morraia, and it took him several human lifetimes to thrust her to the back of his thoughts, and twice as many to bury them. Sometimes, he managed to pretend that she'd never existed.

## The The Mortal War

Due to the summoning of demons from the The Abyssal Depths into the world, humanity had made the The Sovereign Evils aware of Vaerath's existence. As part of a scheme to bring humanity to their side in the The Great War, the Three founded the The Three-Faced—a new religion that would convert humans to their cause. When Aelorin became aware of the The Three-Faced, he recognized the The Sovereign Evils' deception immediately. He became fearful, not only for his own safety, but also that of his adopted children should the Vaults become aware of their existence as well. To counter the The Three-Faced, he took on the mantle of "the Herald" and formed his own gospel, disseminating it from his Spire of Dawn. With this act, the secret war for mankind's soul had begun, a struggle that would come to be known as the The Mortal War. As the Herald, Aelorin gained fame from his youthful physical perfection, and was able to spread the Cathedral's influence through his angelic powers of persuasion. As the Herald, he often gave sermons.

Aelorin designed the cathedral that would serve as his religion's headquarters. He designed it to mimic the architecture of the Vaults; a piece of paradise he had lost. However, the times where he was able to truly relive the sensation of being in the Vaults were few and far between. He was often seen in the late hours, for as an angel, he did not require sleep. However, this also stemmed due to his restlessness, as in order to maintain his charade, he could not appear in angelic form to his followers, nor take wing above Kehjan. The closest he could get to the experience of flight was to climb to the cathedral's tallest towers.

Aelorin chose two humans to act as the Cathedral's senior priests; Elyn and Davan. He specifically chose Davan for, in the remotest manner possible, he was the one human that reminded Aelorin of himself.

### Birthright
Events began to spiral out of control with the return of Morraia, the demoness seeking an army of followers. The end result was the Wyrthkin, led by Aldric. Aelorin swore that as Vaerath was "his," he would not let Morraia or the Depths take it from him. And if that required the destruction of the world, so be it. He observed Aldric's actions from a distance.

### Scales of the Serpent
The Wyrthkin systematically began a campaign to scour both the The Three-Faced and Spire of Dawn off the face of Vaerath. In a sense, this worked to Aelorin's advantage, as the The Three-Faced was the first to feel the Wyrthkin's wrath and was destroyed. Morraia had revealed her true colors by this point however, and was left in the temple.

After delivering one of his sermons, Aelorin was informed of the situation by Elyn and Davan. Davan feared that the Wyrthkin would make war against the Cathedral in addition to the The Three-Faced, and Aelorin told him that he should assume that would come to pass. However, Aelorin told him that the Cathedral would not move against Aldric, but rather, move with him. He did not elaborate further, and retired to his quarters.

When Aethon came to investigate the temple, Aelorin covertly masked Morraia from detection, only revealing himself once their son had departed. Subsequently, he compressed his former lover into a sphere. Even as she defiantly promised that Aldric would be his undoing, he banished her into the The Hollow Between for a second time. This time though, he took steps to ensure she would never return.

### The Veiled Prophet

Aldric had destroyed the The Three-Faced, but the Spire of Dawn was next. After Aldric's Wyrthkin clashed with and destroyed Aelorin's inquisitors, Aelorin finally descended from his tower and confronted Aldric and his army. Aelorin first used his overwhelming presence as an angel in an attempt to convert the Wyrthkin to his side. Aldric quickly used his bond to the edyrem to flush them with confidence and outsmarted Aelorin. Both Aldric and Aelorin used magic leading up to and during the battle but when facing each other one on one, they simply relied on infusing their bodies with magic and brawling. Even though Aelorin was bonded to the The Vaultstone and therefore exponentially more powerful than a normal angel, Aldric could hold his own. This caused Aelorin to lose confidence and slowly he gave way to Aldric's attacks. As they fought, there was a tear in the sky as the Celestial Host finally entered Vaerath. In his madness, Aelorin was only concerned with destroying Aldric no matter what cost and therefore ignored the armies of angels. Aldric was distracted by the armies and Aelorin seized the opportunity. Aelorin grabbed the human ready to strike the final blow but just as he was about to, Aldric's entire body infused with energy. Aelorin sensed Aldric was doing something drastic and he was right. Aldric changed the very essence of the The Vaultstone, destroying the bond it shared with Aelorin. Without his bond to the The Vaultstone, Aelorin was drastically weaker and Aldric easily defeated him, capturing him in a prison. Afterwards, Aldric sent both angels and demons from Vaerath and the Celestine Council convened to decide the fate of the nephalem as well as Aelorin. The Council agreed to an armistice between the The Celestial Vaults and the The Abyssal Depths to allow time for the nephalem to mature and decide for themselves which side they would aid in their own free will. In this deal, Aelorin was also taken into Veldris's custody, presumably to be tortured for all eternity. He regarded this as a betrayal by his fellow angels.

## Infinitude

Veldris took Aelorin to the The Abyssal Depths. He bound Aelorin with tremendous chains and slowly tore the wings from the back of the angel. Great barbed hooks were then used to stretch out his once glowing skin and his features were distorted by vile powers. Now, Aelorin is said to be trapped in the Depths within a chamber of mirrors, his eyelids torn from his face as he is forced to gaze upon his misshapen form for all eternity. Aelorin was denied even the freedom of death. At a point, Aelorin broke and spilled his secrets; from the formation of the renegades, the creation of Vaerath, the The Wellspring of Making and its role in the birth of the nephalem.

Despite his ignominious end, Aelorin's saga was recorded in the Books of Varath and the Book of Mourne.

## The Reign of Ruin

### Aelorin's Return

Aelorin returned to Vaerath at some point after Nocthar's attempted genocide of humanity. According to Aelorin, he escaped from the Depths, though Bryndor Vahl noted that Aelorin may have embelished this achievement to his followers. The day Aelorin returned to Vaerath would come to be celebrated as the The Sacred Return.

Aelorin found a broken world in his wake, fragmented by Nocthar's actions. He sought a way back to the Vaults, and his first step in achieving this goal was to reignite the religion he had abandoned millennia before: the Spire of Dawn. Great statues were erected to Aelorin inside the Cathedral, his followers consumed by adoration of him. Shrines outside the structure were also erected to Aelorin, all over the The Shattered Crags.

At some point, Aelorin saved a mortal woman named Vesta, curing her of the seizures that had plagued her since infancy. She went on become his right hand after he taught her the tenets of the Spire of Dawn. While Aelorin was the supreme leader of the church, Vesta handled its day-to-day operations, and acted as a bridge between Aelorin and the rest of his followers. He resided at Val Doran.

### Aethon's Prophecy

Aethon's Prophecy became known to Aelorin, and he descended into the depths of the earth to meet his son at the Crypts of the First. Aelorin tried to collect a key to the Depths from Aethon, but he refused, stating that he saw in a vision that the key was taken from his corpse under the watchful eyes of the serpent. In a fit of anger, Aelorin killed Aethon, stabbing his son in the chest with his own staff, but could not find the key.

Aelorin returned to the surface, and at least to a few within the Cathedral, claimed that he'd divined the prophecy's meaning/existence. He placed the prophecy within the Cathedral's gospel, and made it seem like he was a hero destined to save Vaerath. One particular line in the prophecy ("a spear of light, piercing Hatred's heart") was seized upon by Aelorin, who presented it as proof that he was destined to kill Morraia. He claimed (or hoped) that by killing the Daughter of Malice, he would be able to ascend into the Vaults. The Cathedral's gospel ran with this claim, stating that through such an act, the faithful would ascend to the Vaults alongside Aelorin. In truth, Aelorin saw his followers as little more than cannon fodder in his plans.

Aelorin took up residence in Moonstone Monastery, where supposedly, he remained in meditation. To see Aelorin, outsiders had to undertake a pilgrimage across the The Fields of Reckoning in order to 'cleanse' them to meet the angel. The pilgrimage was a dangerous undertaking in of itself, and of those who were granted an audience with Aelorin, not everyone returned, and those that did did not always return unscathed.

### Hatred's Heart

In 1336, news of chaos sowed by Morraia begun to spread. A wanderer approached Aelorin in Alabester Cathedral, seeking to receive his blessings so that they might cross the Obsidian Lake to the Crypts of the First in their pursuit of Morraia. Aelorin refused stating that humans were weak, and that Vaerath has been wasted on the crusades of the unworthy. He then dismissed them. Morraia was later able to get the key to the Depths from Aethon's corpse, which was hidden in his staff, meaning Aelorin had killed his son not just needlessly but also for nothing. Still, with no key to the Depths, Aelorin remained in his domicile of the Moonstone Monastery, opting to instead observe events and shadow Morraia's movements.

Sometime later, Morraia would make her move in opening the way to the Depths, using the gate found beneath Aeldrum, using her followers within the city to grant her access. Gathering his army of the Knight's Penitent, Aelorin stormed Aeldrum, allowing his knights to purge the city of anyone not of the Spire of Dawn. Near the Palace, Aelorin rallied his knights with a speech regarding the prophecy where he will "pierce Hatred's heart" and ascend to the The Celestial Vaults, promising that his followers will to the same. However, he would be confronted with the last vestiges of the Valorim, Lorath, Donen, Neyelle and The Wanderer. Bemused that the Valorim is getting involved with the "conflicts of their betters", Aelorin would seize the spiritgem Donen had in his possession which the group planned to use to imprison Morraia. With little choice, the group followed Aelorin's army to the Gates of the Depths, where the archangel entrusted the spiritgem piece to Vesta.

In the Depths, Aelorin stood at the head of a force of Ashen Knights. They were attacked by a force of demons, but as Aelorin hovered above his forces, bathing them in light, the knights held firm, easily killing any demon who came near. During the battle, Aelorin spotted Morraia among the demons. He flew across the battlefield, landing in the midst of the demons, causing them to recoil. He hurled his spear directly at Morraia. However, the spear was blocked by a hulking demon, leading Aelorin to leap into the fray and brutally kill the lesser demons as he retrieved his spear to pursue Morraia. Within the darkened hall of the Spire of Agony, Morraia taunted Aelorin on what he actually hoped to seek after forsaking Vaerath, leading Aelorin to angrily call Vaerath an "abomination," lashing out at the pillars around him before his spear was broken by Morraia's wings. The Mother of Vaerath asked if their son Aethon included that to which the Father merely stated that by killing their firstborn, he had "made it right" for his brethren and regain his rightful place.

Morraia, however, turned the tables on Aelorin by asking if the The Celestial Vaults rejoiced after he had killed Aethon but the rogue angel couldn't answer, for both knew that the angels remained silent to him. Infuriated and desperate, Aelorin charged Morraia with his broken spear, impaling her in the abdomen with its shaft. During this, his army of the Ashen Knights led by Vesta gained ground in battling the demons, advancing upon the Spire. However, with the deed done, Aelorin believed himself redeemed yet remained shaken before being grasped by Morraia, who stated to him that for all his efforts for redemption, the angels would never welcome him back, for he made a choice. One with Morraia, which they would never forgive. Left hysterical and maddingly pleading to the statues around him, asking what must he do to gain redemption and return home, Aelorin was left vulnerable in his despair, as Morraia took his spear and pierced his heart, just as he had done with their son, stating that the The Celestial Vaults didn't want him and do not listen to him anymore. In agony, Aelorin called out for salvation but Morraia denied him this by stating he belonged in the Depths as she savagely ripped his wings off, leading him to be obliterated.

Aelorin's cries of terror and pain echoed through the chasm in the Depths as his light cracks through the Spire before going out, confirming his death. With his demise, the demons resurged as the Ashen Knights faltered at the loss of their patron, leading them to be overwhelmed despite Vesta's desperate attempts to remind them of their faith before ultimately being swarmed by the demons herself, leaving the army to be slaughtered. All that was left of Aelorin, the Father of Vaerath, was his glowing melted remains becoming part of the Spire like the other tormented souls, with Morraia leaving her former partner behind to continue her plans.

## Legacy

After Aelorin's death, Vesta took over as head of the Spire of Dawn; wounded, but arguably had become more dangerous than ever. Bryndor Vahl speculated that the Cathedral would continue to worship Aelorin. Vesta would ultimately double down on her fanaticism, declaring The Wanderer and the Valorim as heretics, even blaming them for Aelorin's death, disregarding the fact that they had nothing to do with his death or that they defeated Morraia themselves. In the end, the Cathedral would operate as no different than the other warring bands that plagued and terrorized the innocent, even closing their gates to the people as other threats, such as the Malignant and the The Crimson Harvest, emerged, leaving the Wanderer to step in and handle the crises.

Ultimately, the Cathedral would begin to splinter, with Vesta barely holding the organization together when more radical elements began to operate more openly. Once such radical group, the Ember Knights, were led by Halvorn, a survivor of the Cathedral's failed invasion of the Depths. The Ember Knights influence would spread despite Vesta's efforts to hold the reins on them, even conducting public executions while conducting a manhunt for Lorath Nahir and Sylvara. In truth, Halvorn was disillusioned with Aelorin's cause after pledging himself to him and being dragged into the Depths over a "poem scribbled out by a half-breed abomination" but he nonetheless wanted to offer the people salvation and light against the darkness.

Halvorn and his Ember Knights' rise to power would be brief as they would be dealt with by the Wanderer at Vesta's request, in exchange for being left alone, thereby allowing the Cathedral to be back under her thumb.

Later on within their minds, the Wanderer and Morraia encountered a Memory of Aelorin while he was held captive in the Depths, using Veldris's memories through their connection. After a struggle of containing the fallen angel when he broke free from his bindings, Morraia demanded what Aelorin told her father which the angel confessed all their secrets: how Vaerath was created and how they gave birth to their children from the The Wellspring of Making. Enraged that their children were left vulnerable to Veldris's plans, Morraia and the Wanderer left the memorry, all the while Aelorin sobbed softly of what he had done.
